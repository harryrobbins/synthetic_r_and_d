## 6. Costs: methodology and schedules

### 6.1 Scope and period

This section sets out how the qualifying expenditure in the claim was identified, apportioned and totalled, and how the relief has been calculated. The claim covers the accounting period from 1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026. The National Insurance basis is 2025-26, and the claim is made under the merged scheme. Expenditure is shown as claimed. The schedules below reproduce the cost schedule for the claim in full.

### 6.2 Identification of qualifying costs

Qualifying costs were identified from Keystone project codes designated as R&D (Growth Fund codes GF-* and associated delivery codes), extracted via the TAX_RD_EXTRACT process and reviewed against the prior year. The codes are listed in the Keystone code list KS-RD-2526, which is held in the claim file. Each code is linked to a Growth Fund paper or to a delivery code agreed with the Growth Fund panel, so that every cost in the extract can be traced to a project in section 5.

The extract was run in April 2026 once the period had closed. The review of the extract was a comparison with the totals for the prior year, which were consistent with the totals in the extract, and the comparison was recorded in the claim file. The review confirmed that the codes in the extract were the codes designated for the period and that the totals were of the expected order.

### 6.3 Staff costs: basis of apportionment

Staff costs comprise salary, employer National Insurance contributions and employer pension contributions for each person or group named in the schedule. The total cost for each line is the sum of those three elements. The claimed amount for each line is the total cost multiplied by the R&D percentage.

Each R&D percentage is the average of Hourglass timesheet bookings to the Keystone codes listed in section 6.2 across the period. Bookings are recorded weekly by each person, and the average is taken across all the weeks in the period in which the person booked time to a code. For the two groups of engineers, the average is taken across the team, so that each group's percentage reflects the bookings of its members as a whole. Where a person booked time to an R&D code for the purposes of programme oversight, the time is included in the average on the same basis as time booked to a project.

Where a percentage is stated for a named team of full-time equivalents, the team's average percentage applies to the total cost of the team. The team figures for the Applied AI and Automation team, for the Digital Delivery team and for the hydraulic modelling and mechanical engineering groups are shown in the schedule on that basis.

The percentages for named individuals are as follows. Scott Lam, Head of Research and Innovation, leads the Growth Fund and oversees the firm's R&D projects, and his percentage reflects that role. Dermot McGregor's percentage reflects his time on P1 and P4. Ewa Walsh's percentage reflects her technical direction of P2 and P3. Ian Ritchie's percentage reflects his time as competent professional and lead data scientist on P2.

### 6.5 Other qualifying expenditure: basis of each category

**Externally provided workers, connected, UK.** Tarnbeck Modelling Services Ltd, a subsidiary of the group, provided surrogate-model modellers to the P2 programme during the period. The claim is for the recharge of £482,000 invoiced to Excelcion for the time those modellers spent on the surrogate programme. The provider cost of the same staff time for the period was £455,000. The recharge was set by the group's intra-group services agreement and is shown in the schedule at £482,000.

**Externally provided workers, connected, overseas.** Halvorsen Pike Engineers Inc., a group company in the United States, provided data scientists who evaluated the approved assistant on North American design codes for P1. The cost of their work is claimed at £310,000. The work was carried out in the period from 12 January 2026 to 31 March 2026 and is recorded in the P1 log in section 5.9.

**Software.** Enterprise licences for the approved AI assistant were provisioned for 1,200 seats under procurement order PROC-2025-0918. The licence cost for the period is claimed at £540,000, which is the full cost of the licences in use by the first cohort and the Applied AI and Automation team.

**Data and cloud.** Lighthouse data platform cloud consumption for P4 is claimed at £720,000. The consumption is recorded against the Lighthouse delivery codes and reconciled to the cloud provider's invoices for the period.

**Consumables.** The immersion-cooling test rig components and dielectric fluid used on P3 are claimed at £38,000. The purchase orders and delivery notes for the components and the fluid are held in the claim file.

**Subcontracted expenditure, unconnected, UK.** The University of Weardale hydrology group prepared benchmark datasets for P2 under a subcontract. The subcontract fee is £60,000. The claim is for 65% of the fee, which reflects the share of the benchmark dataset preparation that relates to qualifying work on the surrogate models. The remaining 35% relates to work on the hydrology group's own programme and is not claimed.

### 6.8 Relief computation

The claim is made under the merged scheme as a large company, and the relief is claimed as a 20% expenditure credit. The credit is taxable and is recognised above the line in the company's accounts for the financial year 2025/26. The net benefit is the expenditure credit less the corporation tax at 25% on that credit.

| Line | Calculation | Amount |
|---|---|---:|
| Total qualifying expenditure | Section 6.7 | £5,046,305 |
| Expenditure credit | 20% of £5,046,305 | £1,009,261 |
| Corporation tax on the credit | 25% of £1,009,261 | £252,315 |
| **Net benefit** | £1,009,261 less £252,315 | **£756,946** |

The net benefit is equivalent to 15% of the total qualifying expenditure. The company's turnover for the prior financial year, 2024/25, was £648,000,000, and the claim is made in the ordinary course of the company's research programme.
