## 5. Technical narrative: P3 (two-phase immersion cooling heat rejection)

### 5.28 P3 overview

**Project reference and status.** P3 is carried out under the Growth Fund's dense-compute cooling programme, with the rig work recorded to the Keystone project code for the programme and the associated delivery codes for the mechanical engineering group. The competent professional is Kenneth Norris, Associate Director, Buildings Mechanical Engineering. The project was supported by Ewa Walsh, Group Technical Director, in her capacity as the firm's technical lead for building services.

**Summary.** High-density compute halls, such as those serving data processing and high-performance computing, produce a large heat load in a small area. The heat has to be removed continuously and the way it is removed determines the structure, plant space, energy use and resilience of the building. Two-phase immersion cooling, in which electronic equipment is submerged in a dielectric fluid that boils at the surface of the hot components, is one of the strategies being considered for the highest densities. The heat is removed by the vapour leaving the fluid, which is then condensed and returned. Whether this strategy can be sized reliably at the design load depends on the boiling heat-transfer coefficient of the fluid at the relevant heat flux.

### 5.29 Baseline

The baseline is the set of published correlations for boiling heat-transfer coefficients in dielectric fluids. These correlations were developed for lower heat fluxes than those now proposed for the highest-density halls, and they were calibrated against a limited set of fluids and surface conditions. The designer who must size the condenser and the heat rejection plant for a new hall has to choose a correlation. At the densities of interest the correlations give different answers, and there is no accepted basis on which to choose between them.

The consequence is that the heat rejection plant is sized conservatively, which carries a cost in capital and in energy use for the life of the building, or that it is sized on a correlation that may not represent the fluid at the relevant density. Neither outcome is satisfactory for a client who needs a reliable design.

### 5.30 Advance sought

The advance sought was a set of measured boiling heat-transfer coefficients for a dielectric fluid at elevated heat fluxes, obtained from a rig that reproduces the surface and fluid conditions of an immersion tank. The advance was intended to show which of the available correlations, if any, represents the measured behaviour at the relevant densities, and to give a design approach that carries the measured coefficients into the selection of a cooling strategy for a high-density hall.

### 5.31 Technical uncertainties

**Uncertainty 1: correlation fit at elevated densities.** It was not known which of the published correlations, if any, would represent the boiling heat-transfer coefficient at the highest heat fluxes considered for the halls.

**Uncertainty 2: scale.** A rig measures the coefficient on a heated surface of limited size. It was not known whether the coefficient measured at rig scale would apply to the larger surfaces and the flow conditions of a full-scale immersion tank.

**Uncertainty 3: design application.** It was not known whether a strategy approach based on the measured coefficients could be applied across the range of design loads that clients specify, or whether the coefficients would need to be measured again for each fluid.

### 5.32 Rig and methodology

The rig is a closed loop in which a dielectric fluid is held at saturation pressure and heated by an electrically heated test surface. The test surface is a copper block with embedded thermocouples, and the heat flux is controlled by the electrical input. The vapour generated at the surface rises to a condenser, where it is cooled and returned to the test chamber. The rig is instrumented for the surface temperature, the saturation temperature, the heat input, the condenser duty and the fluid pressure. Each measurement records the steady state reached at a given heat flux.

Commissioning took place in April and May 2025 in the dedicated laboratory at the Manchester office. Commissioning included calibration of the thermocouples, an energy balance check on the condenser and a repeatability test on the test surface. Repeatability was within the uncertainty stated for the instrumentation in the project file.

Measurements were taken on test sheets XC-IMM-01 to XC-IMM-22. Each test sheet records a sequence of heat flux steps, the steady state at each step, the coefficient derived from the surface temperature and the heat flux, and the uncertainty on that coefficient. The steps were taken at increasing heat flux, and a run was accepted only when the surface temperature had been steady for the period recorded on the sheet.

The coefficients were compared with the published correlations at each heat flux. The comparison was made for each correlation separately, and the divergence between correlations at the highest heat fluxes was recorded in the test sheet summary. The method is standard in the experimental literature on boiling and is a conventional approach to resolving uncertainty 1.

### 5.33 Application to cooling strategy

The measured coefficients were used to inform cooling strategy studies for high-density halls. The mechanical engineering group, which comprises nine full-time equivalent engineers, applied the coefficients to the condenser sizing and to the plant arrangement for the strategy options, following the firm's published guidance on the choice of cooling strategy for dense computing. The guidance is set out in the Insights article Cooling dense computing: choose the strategy before the building, published on 10 June 2025 by Kenneth Norris. The article describes the decision points the group uses to select a strategy, including the design heat load, the range of loads and the plant space each strategy requires.

Design studies for clients' data-centre commissions made use of the rig results in this way. The studies were delivered under the appointments in place with those clients and billed under the terms of those appointments. The time the group spent on those studies is recorded to the P3 Keystone code in proportion to the research content of the work, and the averages are set out in section 6.

The application of the coefficients to strategy selection is part of P3 because it tests uncertainty 3 directly. The studies showed that the measured coefficients change the condenser sizing at the highest densities, which is the outcome the advance was designed to test.

### 5.35 Outcome of P3 in the accounting period

The rig has produced measured boiling heat-transfer coefficients across the range of heat fluxes recorded in XC-IMM-01 to XC-IMM-22. The measured coefficients diverge from the published correlations at the highest heat fluxes, and the divergence is recorded and quantified in the summary. The coefficients were applied to cooling strategy studies, which showed a change in the condenser sizing at the highest loads.

Uncertainty 1 is resolved in the sense that the correlations have been compared on measured data. Uncertainty 2 is open, because the scale question requires a full-scale test that has not been carried out, and it is noted as a question for future work. Uncertainty 3 is partly resolved, because the strategy approach has been applied to one concept, and it will be applied to others as the group's programme continues.

### 5.36 Boundaries of P3

The qualifying work on P3 is the work carried out between 1 April 2025 and 31 March 2026. The commissioning in April and May 2025 is within the period. The rig is not used for any work outside the programme.

Within the period, the following activities are excluded from P3 and are not claimed. Routine mechanical design of cooling systems for clients in which the rig results were not used, and the general coordination of mechanical services on client commissions. The engineering design and documentation of the plant for each client commission, which is delivered under the terms of the client appointment and is part of ordinary design practice. The construction and commissioning of the plant for any client, which is not claimed. Design work on client commissions is claimed only to the extent that it applied the measured coefficients, as recorded in section 5.33.

The end of the qualifying work in the period is 31 March 2026.
