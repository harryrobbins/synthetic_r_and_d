### 5.10 Project P2: powder reuse limits for Ti-6Al-4V under high recoat-frequency builds

#### 5.10.1 Background and baseline

Ti-6Al-4V powder that is not consumed in a build is sieved and returned to the powder store for reuse. Each reuse cycle exposes the powder to elevated temperature and to residual oxygen in the build chamber, and the oxygen content of the powder rises with each cycle. Oxygen pickup is significant because it raises the strength and reduces the ductility of the as-built material, and fatigue performance is particularly sensitive to it. Reuse limits for Ti-6Al-4V are therefore set in the standards literature and in the company's powder control procedure.

The baseline for P2 is the reuse limit established for conventional build rates, in which the powder is exposed to the build chamber for a limited time per layer and the recoat frequency is low. Thin-wall builds of the kind used in THINFIN have a high recoat frequency. The recoater passes over the build surface once per layer, and for a 180 mm build of 30 micron layers this is 6,000 passes, of which a large proportion pass over thin walls that are heated by the laser and that lie close to the powder bed. The company's prior experience was that this exposure was not reflected in the conventional reuse limit, but the effect had not been measured.

#### 5.10.2 Advance and uncertainties

The advance sought was a measured reuse limit for thin-wall builds with high recoat frequency, expressed as the maximum number of reuse cycles before the oxygen content of the powder exceeds the level at which fatigue performance is affected. The uncertainties were whether oxygen pickup under high recoat frequency departed from the conventional behaviour, and, if it did, by how much.

#### 5.10.3 Methodology

Powder was sampled from the powder store after each of the first ten reuse cycles of the THINFIN powder. Samples were analysed for oxygen content by inert gas fusion, with the results recorded against the lot number and the build sequence. Tensile specimens were built from the reused powder at cycles 1, 4, 7 and 10, and tested in accordance with the company's internal fatigue and tensile procedure, with the specimens tested at the Midshire AM Test Centre. The results were compared with the conventional reuse data in the powder supplier's technical literature.

#### 5.10.4 Work log

**October 2024 to January 2025.** Powder for cycles 1 to 4 was sampled. The oxygen content rose from 0.13% by weight in the virgin powder to 0.16% at cycle 4, an increase that was in line with the conventional behaviour in the supplier's data.

**February to April 2025.** Powder for cycles 5 to 7 was sampled. The oxygen content at cycle 7 was 0.19% by weight, which was higher than the conventional data would predict for the same number of cycles. The company recorded the difference as a finding of the study and repeated the analysis on a second powder lot.

**May 2025.** The second lot confirmed the higher pickup, with oxygen content at cycle 7 of 0.19% by weight. Tensile specimens from cycles 1, 4 and 7 showed a reduction in elongation at cycle 7 that was consistent with the oxygen content.

**June 2025.** Powder for cycles 8 to 10 was sampled. The oxygen content at cycle 10 was 0.22% by weight. The tensile specimens from cycle 10 met the strength criterion but showed elongation below the fatigue criterion in the customer's specification. The reuse limit was therefore set at seven cycles for thin-wall builds of this type, with a maximum oxygen content of 0.20% by weight, and the limit was recorded in the powder control procedure in June 2025.

#### 5.10.5 Outcome

The outcome of P2 is a measured reuse limit of seven cycles for high recoat-frequency thin-wall builds in Ti-6Al-4V, which is shorter than the limit in the conventional data. The limit is applied to all THINFIN builds from June 2025, and it governed the powder blending ratios used in the validation builds described at section 5.11. The study is a component of the THINFIN programme and was carried out by the process engineer, Sophie Treleaven, with the simulation of powder thermal history carried out by the simulation engineer, Dev Mistry.

### 5.11 Validation builds after resolution of the programme uncertainties

#### 5.11.1 Purpose

The THINFIN uncertainties were resolved on 16 June 2025, when build TPE-BL-422 met the acceptance criteria. Following that date, the company undertook three further builds, TPE-BL-423, TPE-BL-424 and TPE-BL-425, which the company describes as validation builds. Their purpose was to confirm that the resolved process could be reproduced on different operators, different machine conditions and different powder lots, which is the test a competent professional would apply before a process can be regarded as established. The validation builds were produced to the design of the Corran Aero Systems heat exchanger and were run at the reuse limit set under P2.

The company's view is that the validation builds form part of the development of the THINFIN process, because each one tested a question that remained open after June 2025: whether the process was robust to changes in operator, machine state and powder lot.

#### 5.11.2 Work log

**July 2025: TPE-BL-423.** The first validation build, on the first system, run by the machine operator, Gareth Pollock, with the process engineer supervising the set-up and the in-situ layer imaging. The build was run at the production scan strategy with a powder blend containing 42% reused powder at cycle 5. The wall deviation at all 24 control points was within ±0.07 mm and no recoater event was recorded. The operator's supervision and in-process inspection on this build was greater than on the development series because the operator was undertaking his first build on the revised strategy, and time has been recorded at 60% for the period to reflect that.

**August 2025: TPE-BL-424.** The second validation build, on the first system, with a new powder lot. The lot was analysed on receipt, and the oxygen content of the virgin powder was 0.12% by weight. The build was run with the same strategy and blend. The wall deviation was 0.08 mm and CT porosity was 0.15% by volume.

**September 2025: TPE-BL-425.** The third validation build, on the second system, with the powder blend at the reuse limit of seven cycles. The wall deviation was 0.07 mm and CT porosity was 0.18% by volume. The build confirmed the process on the second system at the reuse limit.

The walls from the three validation builds were measured by coordinate measurement and sectioned for CT. Heat-exchanger sections from these builds were supplied to Corran Aero Systems in July, August and September 2025 for use in their qualification trial of the design. The company regards the supply of sections for the customer's qualification trial as a normal part of the validation of a new process.

#### 5.11.3 Outcome

The three validation builds met the acceptance criteria. The process was shown to be reproducible across operators, machines and powder lots within the limits described in section 5.13.

### 5.12 Subcontracted computed tomography and test services

#### 5.12.1 Computed tomography

The company does not operate computed tomography equipment in-house, and all CT scanning of THINFIN builds was carried out by Präzisionsmesstechnik Halde GmbH, an independent metrology laboratory in Germany. The laboratory scanned the walls of the calibration builds, the full-height builds and the validation builds, and it issued a report for each scan in the PMH-CT-2025 series. The reports are listed in the evidence index.

The laboratory was selected in preference to two UK providers. The company's requirement was for scans of thin-wall sections at a voxel size of 25 microns or finer, with a report within ten working days, because each build in the development series was scanned before the next build was designed. The UK providers approached by the company quoted turnaround times of six to eight weeks, which would have prevented the iteration described in section 5.7. The laboratory's turnaround was ten working days, and its price per scan for the volume of scans required across the programme was favourable. The total contract value was £48,000, of which 65% is claimed in the cost schedule as described in section 6.5.

The laboratory's reports include the scan parameters, the reconstructed voxel size and the porosity analysis in each wall. The company's metrology technician, Lisa Okafor, reviewed each report against the coordinate measurement for the same build.

#### 5.12.2 Fatigue testing

Fatigue testing of coupons cut from the calibration and validation builds was carried out by Midshire AM Test Centre, a UK test facility with no connection to the company. The test centre tested the coupons to the company's fatigue procedure, with the results reported against the customer's specification. The contract value was £40,000, of which 65% is claimed.

### 5.13 Outcomes, project boundaries and summary of findings

#### 5.13.1 Outcomes of P1

The principal outcome of P1 is a process for the production of 0.35 mm Ti-6Al-4V walls in plate-fin heat exchangers, with a wall position of ±0.07 mm over a build height of 180 mm and with no internal supports. The process was calibrated by a thermo-mechanical simulation that predicts the wall position to within 0.06 mm at the control points used in the programme. The scan strategy comprises variable contour power, the stitched island orientation and the recoat protocol, and each element was shown to contribute to the result.

The process reproduces across both laser powder-bed fusion systems at the company's site. Porosity is controlled to below 0.3% by volume in the walls, with the energy window set by the porosity and buckling limits. Recoater collision is predicted from the calibrated simulation and is avoided by the recoat protocol.

#### 5.13.2 Boundaries of P1

The project started on 1 October 2024, when the calibration build plan was approved, and the first build of the claimed series was started on 3 October 2024. The project's technical uncertainties were resolved on 16 June 2025. The validation builds of July to September 2025 are described at section 5.11 and are included in the project as the confirmation phase of the process.

#### 5.13.3 Summary of findings

The programme resolved each of the three uncertainties set out at section 5.5. The first was resolved by the calibration series, which was closed in April 2025, and which showed that a thermo-mechanical simulation can be calibrated at 0.35 mm within the limits of the available measurement. The second was resolved by the energy window identified in builds TPE-BL-412 to TPE-BL-418, which controls buckling and porosity together. The third was resolved by the simulation-based prediction of wall position combined with the recoat protocol, which avoided every recoater event in the development series after TPE-BL-420.

The programme's governance records were maintained by the technical review board, which met four times in the period. The Managing Director chaired each meeting in his capacity as programme sponsor, and the minutes of each meeting record the review of the build results and the decisions taken on the next builds. The minutes are retained as programme governance records and are available to HMRC.
