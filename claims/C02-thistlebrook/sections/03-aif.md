### 3.6 Project 1: THINFIN distortion-compensated LPBF of thin-wall heat exchangers (TPE-2024-07)

**Main field of science or technology.** Additive manufacturing process science, specifically the thermo-mechanical modelling, in-situ monitoring and scan-strategy control of laser powder-bed fusion of titanium alloys at wall thicknesses below 0.5 mm.

**Baseline (what was known or readily deducible before the project).** Commercial build-simulation tools can predict macro-distortion for walls of 0.8 mm and above to within about 0.1 mm. Below 0.5 mm, wall buckling and recoater interaction dominate the distortion behaviour and predictive accuracy falls away. Industry practice for thin-walled channels is either to thicken the walls, with a corresponding loss of heat-transfer performance, or to use internal supports that cannot be removed from enclosed channels.

**Advance sought.** A method that predicts and compensates distortion in 0.35 mm Ti-6Al-4V walls, combining calibrated thermo-mechanical simulation with a scan strategy that suppresses buckling, so that plate-fin heat exchangers of 180 mm build height can be made to ±0.06 mm without internal supports.

**Scientific or technological uncertainties.** Whether thermal-stress models can be calibrated at all at this wall thickness, given that the voxel size approaches the melt-pool width. Whether scan strategies that suppress buckling would introduce lack-of-fusion porosity. Whether recoater collision could be predicted from the build geometry before a build is started.

**How and when the uncertainties were addressed.** Through a programme of 22 instrumented builds (TPE-BL-401 to TPE-BL-422) between October 2024 and June 2025, with in-situ layer imaging, computed tomography and fatigue coupon testing at each stage. The calibration uncertainty was resolved when the calibration series was closed in April 2025. The porosity and recoater uncertainties were resolved in June 2025, after the failures recorded on builds TPE-BL-404, TPE-BL-409 and TPE-BL-412 had been analysed and the scan strategy revised.

### 3.7 Project 2: Powder reuse limits for Ti-6Al-4V under high recoat-frequency builds (within TPE-2024-07)

**Main field of science or technology.** Materials science, specifically oxygen pickup and property retention in reused Ti-6Al-4V powder.

**Baseline.** Reuse limits for Ti-6Al-4V powder are established for conventional build rates in the standards literature. The effect of high recoat frequency on oxygen pickup in thin-wall builds was not recorded in the company's prior work.

**Advance sought.** A measured reuse limit for thin-wall builds with high recoat frequency, expressed as a maximum number of reuse cycles before oxygen content exceeds the limit that would affect fatigue performance.

**Uncertainties.** Whether oxygen pickup under high recoat frequency departs from the standard behaviour, and if so by how much.

**How and when resolved.** Oxygen pickup was measured on powder sampled after each of the first ten reuse cycles from the THINFIN builds. The reuse limit was set in June 2025 and recorded in the company's powder control procedure.

### 3.10 Project boundaries

**Start of the R&D project (P1).** The THINFIN project was opened as a formal programme on 1 October 2024, when the calibration build plan TPE-BL-401 was approved. The predecessor titanium thin-wall work, which covered wall thicknesses down to 0.8 mm, ended on 30 September 2024 and is not claimed. The first build in the claimed series was started on 3 October 2024.

**End of the R&D project (P1).** The technical uncertainties of the project were resolved in June 2025, when build TPE-BL-422 confirmed the dimensional and porosity criteria on the revised scan strategy. The company considers the project to have ended at the close of its confirmation phase on 30 September 2025. The three validation builds carried out between July and September 2025 (TPE-BL-423 to TPE-BL-425) confirm the resolved process under changed operators, machines and powder lots, and are included in the project as its confirmation phase, as described at section 5 of this pack.

**Boundaries of the secondary project (P2).** The powder oxygen-pickup series began with the first reuse cycle sampled on 14 October 2024 and the reuse limit was set in June 2025.

**Relationship to other activities.** The company also undertakes CNC subcontract machining for its aerospace and motorsport customers. That activity is outside the scope of this claim and no costs for it are included in the cost schedules.
