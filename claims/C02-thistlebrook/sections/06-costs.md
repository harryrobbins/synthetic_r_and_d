## 6. Cost Methodology and Schedules

### 6.1 Basis of preparation

This section sets out the cost schedules for the accounting period from 1 October 2024 to 30 September 2025 and explains how each figure has been derived. The schedules are prepared from the company's payroll records for the period, using the 2025-26 employer National Insurance basis, the company's supplier invoices and the contract documents for the external services. The claim is made under the merged scheme, and the expenditure credit is taxable.

The schedules reproduce the figures in the company's cost file for the period without alteration. Every figure is shown to the nearest pound, and totals are the sum of the figures shown in each table. The company's accounting records are retained for inspection and are referenced in the evidence index in the appendices.

### 6.3 Apportionment of staff costs

The R&D percentages in the staff schedule were estimated by each employee's line manager at year end, with reference to the build logs for the period. The build-log series records, for each build, the programme code, the operator, the process engineer and the authoring engineer, together with the date of each build and the time spent on set-up, measurement and analysis. The line managers used the build records to estimate the proportion of each employee's working time that was spent on the THINFIN programme, and they recorded the estimates in the year-end staff review, which is retained with the claim papers.

Working time in the additive manufacturing suite is recorded through the build-log system and through attendance records for the metrology bench and the simulation workstation. A monthly extract of those records, presented as a timesheet extract, is included in the appendices. The extract is consistent with the percentages in the schedule, and it has been used to check the reasonableness of each estimate.

The percentage for Dr Callum Ashdown, Head of Additive Manufacturing Engineering, is 75%. He was the authoring engineer for each phase 1 and phase 2 build plan, and the remaining 25% of his time was spent on other additive manufacturing work for customer enquiries and production planning, and on the company's wider engineering management. His percentage was estimated at year end by the Managing Director, to whom he reports.

The percentage for Sophie Treleaven, Process Engineer, is 90%. She set up and supervised the THINFIN builds, analysed the in-situ layer images and led the P2 powder sampling. The remaining time was spent on the production of customer parts on the first system.

The percentage for Dev Mistry, Simulation Engineer, is 90%. He maintained the calibration of the thermo-mechanical model, prepared the predictions for each build and carried out the powder thermal history analysis for P2. The remaining time was spent on the simulation of customer production parts.

The percentage for Gareth Pollock, Machine Operator, is 60%. His time on the programme includes the operation of the development builds and, in the second half of the period, the operation of the three validation builds described at section 5.11 of this pack. The operator's time on those builds was greater than on the development series, because he was carrying out his first build on the revised strategy, and the percentage reflects the additional supervision and in-process inspection that the build required. The remaining 40% of his time was spent on the operation of the production machines for customer parts.

The percentage for Lisa Okafor, Metrology Technician, is 50%. She carried out all coordinate measurement of the THINFIN builds and reviewed the CT reports. The remaining time was spent on the metrology of customer parts.

The percentage for Neil Hambleton, Managing Director, is 25%. The Managing Director is the programme sponsor for THINFIN. He chaired the technical review board, which met four times in the period to review the build results and to decide the next builds, and he took part in the review of each phase of the programme. The remainder of his time was spent on the company's commercial and strategic activity, including customer relationships with Corran Aero Systems and other customers. His percentage was estimated at year end by the Finance Director.

### 6.5 Subcontracted activities

#### 6.5.1 Computed tomography

Computed tomography of THINFIN builds was carried out by Präzisionsmesstechnik Halde GmbH, a metrology laboratory in Germany, under a service contract with a total value of £48,000. The contract covered the scanning of the calibration builds from TPE-BL-405 onwards, the full-height builds and the validation builds, and the laboratory issued a report for each scan in the PMH-CT-2025 series. The laboratory was chosen because its ten-working-day turnaround allowed each build in the development series to be scanned before the next build was designed, and because its per-scan pricing was favourable for the volume of scans required. The contract value is shown in the cost schedule at £31,200, which is 65% of the contract value.

The company's view is that the scanning was an essential part of the work of the programme, since computed tomography was the only method by which the porosity and the rounded wall profile could be measured reliably at 0.35 mm, and the reports were used directly in the calibration.

#### 6.5.2 Fatigue testing

Fatigue testing of coupons from the calibration and validation builds was carried out by Midshire AM Test Centre, a UK test facility. The company has no connection with Midshire AM Test Centre, and the contract was placed on a commercial basis. The contract value was £40,000, and £26,000 (65%) is claimed in the cost schedule.

### 6.6 Consumables and software

#### 6.6.1 Consumables for R&D builds

The first consumables line, £92,000, comprises Ti-6Al-4V powder and build plates consumed in the R&D builds of the THINFIN programme, including the 22 development builds from TPE-BL-401 to TPE-BL-422 and the powder sampled for the P2 reuse study. The powder was purchased from a UK powder supplier in lots of 25 kg, and each lot is recorded against the build log in which it was used. The build plates are wrought Ti-6Al-4V billet plates, one of which is consumed in each build.

#### 6.6.2 Consumables for the post-resolution builds

The second consumables line, £61,500, comprises the Ti-6Al-4V powder consumed in the three builds carried out after the uncertainties were resolved on 16 June 2025, namely TPE-BL-423, TPE-BL-424 and TPE-BL-425. These are the validation builds described at section 5.11 of this pack. The powder for these builds was drawn from the same powder control system as the development builds and was blended to the reuse limit set under P2. The sections built on those validation builds were supplied to Corran Aero Systems for their qualification trial in July, August and September 2025, and the supply is recorded in the company's sales ledger.

#### 6.6.3 Simulation software

The simulation software line, £44,000, comprises the annual licences for the thermo-mechanical build-simulation package used in the THINFIN programme, including the solver, the heat-source calibration module and the distortion post-processing module. The licences are claimed at 100% on the basis that the package is used for the THINFIN programme. The same package also supports the quotation process for new customer enquiries, and the licence cost is not apportioned between those uses in the schedule.

#### 6.6.4 Enterprise and office software

The software line of £9,800 comprises the company's ERP and Microsoft 365 licences. The ERP system records the build logs, the powder control records and the purchase records for the THINFIN programme, and the Microsoft 365 licences support the programme's document and email records, including the programme register and the minutes of the technical review board. The licences are included in full in the schedule as software used in R&D.
