## 4. Company Background

### 4.1 History

Thistlebrook Precision Engineering Ltd was founded in 1987 by Michael Hambleton as a contract CNC machining business serving the automotive and agricultural sectors from a single workshop in Hexley. The company moved to its present site, Thistlebrook Works on Moorgate Lane, in 1994, when the original premises were no longer large enough to accommodate a multi-axis machining centre. Through the 1990s and 2000s the business built a reputation for turning complex steel and aluminium components to tight tolerances, and it became a tier-two supplier to aerospace and industrial gas turbine manufacturers.

The company is now in its second generation of family ownership. Neil Hambleton, the son of the founder, joined the business as a production engineer in 1999, became Operations Director in 2011 and was appointed Managing Director in 2016. Michael Hambleton remains a non-executive director and is consulted on major capital investments.

### 4.2 Current business

Thistlebrook currently employs 96 people and reports turnover of £14.8 million for the year ended 30 September 2025. The business is organised around three operating areas:

- **Precision subcontract machining.** Five-axis and turning work for aerospace tier-two suppliers and for motorsport and industrial gas turbine customers. This remains the largest part of the business by revenue.
- **Additive manufacturing.** Laser powder-bed fusion production and development, based in a dedicated suite on the north side of the site. This operation has been developed since 2019 and is the focus of the company's research and development activity.
- **Metrology and inspection.** Coordinate measuring, surface finish measurement and, from 2023, in-house computed tomography (CT) data processing for components produced on site.

Customers include Corran Aero Systems, a fictional tier-one aerospace supplier for whom the company machines turbine casing flanges, and a number of motorsport and industrial gas turbine organisations that are not named in this pack for commercial reasons.

### 4.3 Additive manufacturing investment

The company's entry into additive manufacturing followed a 2018 review of its customers' requirements for lightweight internal-channel components. Customers had begun to ask for heat exchangers and manifolds that could not be machined because their internal geometries were not accessible to cutting tools. In 2019 the company commissioned its first laser powder-bed fusion system, a machine with a 250 mm by 250 mm build plate and a single fibre laser, and it installed a powder handling and sieving room alongside it.

A second system with a larger build volume was commissioned in 2021. Since then, the additive manufacturing suite has been equipped with an in-line powder recycling station, an inert-gas glovebox, a pre-build simulation workstation and a dedicated metrology bench for coordinate measurement of additively manufactured parts.

### 4.4 Ownership and group structure

Thistlebrook Precision Engineering Ltd is not part of a group. It is owned by the Hambleton Family Trust (45%), Neil Hambleton (30%), and Michael Hambleton (25%). The company has no subsidiaries, and it has no overseas parent or overseas branch. The company holds no shares in any other trading company.

The company's accounts are prepared in-house and audited by an external firm. The Finance Director, Karen Ashby ACA, joined the company in 2017 from a regional accountancy practice and is responsible for statutory reporting, management accounts and tax compliance.

### 4.5 Funding

The company has been profitable in each of the last five years. The additive manufacturing investment has been funded from retained earnings and from a term loan of £2.1 million secured against the Moorgate Lane site in 2019. The term loan was drawn in two tranches and is being repaid over ten years. No grant or other public funding has been received for the R&D activities claimed in this pack.

The company's profit before tax for the period ended 30 September 2025 was approximately £1.1 million. The Board's view is that the additive manufacturing programme is now self-funding at the level of its current production volumes, although further development expenditure continues to be funded from the company's own resources.

### 4.6 Staff and organisation

At 30 September 2025 the company had 96 employees, of whom 74 were based at Thistlebrook Works and the remainder were in the sales, administration and quality functions. The R&D activity claimed is carried out within the additive manufacturing engineering function, which is led by Dr Callum Ashdown as Head of Additive Manufacturing Engineering. The function had six people engaged directly on the programme during the period: the competent professional, a process engineer, a simulation engineer, a machine operator, a metrology technician, and the Managing Director in his role as programme sponsor.

Dr Ashdown joined the company in 2022. He holds an EngD in additive manufacturing process modelling and has nine years' experience of laser powder-bed fusion process development. Before joining Thistlebrook he worked at a national additive manufacturing catapult centre, where he led a programme on distortion in thin-walled titanium structures.

### 4.7 Facilities

The additive manufacturing suite at Thistlebrook Works comprises:

- two laser powder-bed fusion systems, each with inert-gas atmosphere control and in-situ layer imaging;
- a powder handling room with sieving, blending and in-line recycling equipment;
- a metrology bench with a coordinate measuring machine, a surface profilometer and an optical microscope;
- a pre-build simulation workstation running thermo-mechanical build-simulation software;
- a laboratory area with specimen preparation equipment and a hardness tester.

Computed tomography was not available in-house during the claim period. CT scans were therefore obtained from external providers, as described in the cost methodology in section 6. Mechanical fatigue testing was also undertaken externally, at the facilities of a UK test centre.

### 4.8 Quality and certification

The company holds AS9100 and ISO 9001 certification, and the additive manufacturing suite operates under a quality procedure that was extended to powder control and build records in 2022. All R&D builds in the period were recorded in the build-log system under the programme code TPE-2024-07, and each build log is linked to the laboratory book page or metrology record generated for it.

### 4.9 Prior R&D activity

The company has made R&D tax claims in each accounting period since 2016. Earlier claims covered build-parameter development for Inconel 718 and AlSi10Mg on the first laser powder-bed fusion system, and the development of a fixture-free approach to the production of manifolds. Those projects are summarised in the prior-year technical summaries held by the company and available to HMRC on request. The THINFIN project is the continuation of the titanium thin-wall work begun in the 2023-24 period and is described in the technical narrative.
