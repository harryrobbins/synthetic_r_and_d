### 3.6 Project 1

**Project title:** Hallam Road deep basement: innovative retaining solution in challenging ground conditions

**Main field of science or technology:** Civil and structural engineering, specifically ground engineering, temporary works and deep basement construction.

**Baseline:**

The baseline was the Company's established practice for basement retention in comparable urban terraced sites, together with the general industry practice understood by experienced groundworks teams at the time the project was scoped. Existing practice was not able to give confidence about the performance of the retaining wall in the ground conditions found at Hallam Road.

**Technological advance sought:**

The Company sought to advance its enhanced capability in deep basement construction. In particular, the Company sought a retaining solution for a two-storey basement beneath a Victorian terrace that could be built safely within a high water table and made ground, while keeping movement of the adjacent party wall within acceptable limits.

**Technological uncertainties:**

The principal uncertainties were whether the adjacent party wall would remain stable during excavation, and the nature of the unknown ground conditions beneath and around the excavation. Neither could be readily deduced by a competent professional from desk study information, and both bore directly on the design and sequencing of the temporary works.

**Systematic approach adopted:**

Throughout the claim period the Company sought to achieve an appreciable improvement in the overall knowledge and capability in its field, resolving uncertainties that could not be readily deduced by a competent professional working in the field, in accordance with paragraphs 3, 6 and 13 of the DSIT Guidelines.

The Company installed an inclinometer and groundwater monitoring array before excavation, took baseline readings, and set amber and red movement triggers. Designs and temporary works were iteratively developed, tested against defined success criteria, and refined based on observed performance. Each propping stage was reviewed against the monitoring record before the next excavation stage was released.

**Outcome:**

The basement was excavated to formation level and the structure completed within the accounting period. Movement of the party wall remained within the agreed triggers throughout. The Company has gained documented knowledge of secant pile performance in made ground with a high water table, and of the sequencing of temporary propping that it now applies to comparable basements.

### 3.7 Project 2

**Project title:** Digital transformation of site operations

**Main field of science or technology:** Information and communications technology, specifically construction information systems, site data capture and building information modelling.

**Baseline:**

The baseline was the Company's existing paper-based site diary, photographic records and manual survey practice, together with the typical practice of groundworks contractors of the Company's size at the time the project began.

**Technological advance sought:**

The Company sought to advance its capability to manage site information digitally by integrating a cloud-based site diary application, drone survey capture and a BIM viewer into a single site information flow. The aim was an improvement in the accuracy, timeliness and retrievability of site records across concurrent groundworks projects.

**Technological uncertainties:**

The principal technological uncertainty was whether site teams would adopt the technology in daily use, given the varied working patterns, mixed device access and limited digital experience across the operative workforce. Whether the integrated data flow would function reliably on remote sites with limited connectivity was also uncertain.

**Systematic approach adopted:**

Designs and workflows were iteratively developed, tested against defined success criteria, and refined based on observed performance. Pilot use on Hallam Road and two further sites was reviewed at fixed intervals, and changes to the configuration and training were recorded in the project log.

**Outcome:**

Adoption was confirmed at a level sufficient for the Company to continue to use the site diary and BIM viewer on all live sites. The Company has documented the configuration and training approach that achieved adoption, and has a tested basis for site information management on future projects.
