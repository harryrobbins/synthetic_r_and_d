## 5. Technical Narrative: Introduction and Project P1 (Hallam Road)

### 5.1 Purpose and scope

This section sets out the technical narrative for the research and development activities undertaken by Brackenmoor Groundworks Ltd (the Company) in the accounting period 1 August 2024 to 31 July 2025. It is written project by project. For each project it describes the baseline, the technological advance sought, the technological uncertainties, the systematic approach adopted, a dated work log of iterations, the outcomes, and the start and end boundaries of the work. A competent professional statement is given at the end of each project, and a statement on the Company's overall approach is given at Section 5.12.

The narrative is intended to be read with the Additional Information Form at Section 3, which contains the summary fields for each project, and with the cost schedules at Section 6.

Throughout the claim period the Company sought to achieve an appreciable improvement in the overall knowledge and capability in its field, resolving uncertainties that could not be readily deduced by a competent professional working in the field, in accordance with paragraphs 3, 6 and 13 of the DSIT Guidelines.

### 5.2 Terms used in this narrative

"Baseline" means the level of knowledge and capability available to a competent professional in the Company's field at the start of the project, which the Company drew upon in scoping the work.

"Advance" means the improvement in knowledge or capability that the Company sought to achieve. It is distinct from the commercial outcome of any individual contract.

"Uncertainty" means a technological question that could not be resolved by a competent professional using existing knowledge and practice, and which therefore required investigation.

"Monitoring trigger" means a threshold for measured ground or structural movement, set before excavation, at which the site team was required to stop, review and, where necessary, adjust the temporary works.

"Competent professional" means a person with the knowledge, qualifications and experience of an experienced practitioner in the Company's field, as described in Section 5.8.

### 5.3 Project P1: Hallam Road deep basement

**Project title:** Hallam Road deep basement: innovative retaining solution in challenging ground conditions.

**Site description.** The project site is a Victorian terrace on Hallam Road, Sheffield. The client's brief was to construct a two-storey basement beneath the existing house, providing habitable space at lower ground level. The house forms part of a continuous terrace, and the party walls on both sides carry the load of the adjoining houses on shallow foundations. Excavation to the proposed formation level would take the base of the new basement well below the foundations of the party walls.

**Ground conditions.** The desk study identified a thin layer of made ground over glacial deposits, with a high water table close to the existing ground level. Historic records indicated former industrial use on the adjacent plot, but the extent and character of any made ground beneath the party walls could not be confirmed from the information available before the works were let. The ground conditions beneath and around the excavation were therefore unknown in their detail at the time of design.

**Client contract.** The works were let under a JCT design-and-build variant on a fixed-price basis of £1.38 million. The client's design team appointed the structural and temporary works designers. The Company was responsible for the construction of the basement, including the temporary works, the retaining structure as built, the monitoring arrangements during construction and the coordination of the construction programme.

The design of the retaining solution was carried out by Pennine Structural Consultants, who were appointed by the client's design team at RIBA Stage 3 and novated to the Company under the design-and-build contract; their fees, which were recovered within the fixed contract sum, are included in the subcontract costs at Section 6. The design-development outputs were reviewed by the Company's site engineering team, which then developed the detailed temporary works sequence and the monitoring arrangements that are the subject of this claim.

### 5.4 Project P1: Baseline

The baseline for P1 was the Company's established practice for basement retention in comparable urban terraced sites, together with the general industry practice understood by experienced groundworks teams. In the Company's experience, retaining walls in terraced housing are commonly formed using contiguous or secant piles, with a temporary propping sequence installed as excavation proceeds, and with ground and building movement monitored against trigger levels.

That practice was not sufficient, on its own, to give confidence in the performance of the wall at Hallam Road. The combination of a high water table, made ground of uncertain character, and a party wall with a shallow foundation meant that the response of the ground and the adjacent structure to staged excavation could not be predicted with confidence from existing practice. The baseline therefore provided the starting point for the investigation, but did not resolve the questions on which the claim is based.

### 5.5 Project P1: Technological advance sought

The Company sought to advance its enhanced capability in deep basement construction. Specifically, it sought:

(a) a retaining solution for a two-storey basement beneath a Victorian terrace, formed within a high water table and made ground, that could be built without unacceptable movement of the adjacent party wall;

(b) a temporary propping and excavation sequence, tied to measured movement, that could be applied to comparable basements in similar ground, and that the Company could specify and control on site without reliance on repeated external redesign; and

(c) a monitoring and decision process that allowed excavation to be released stage by stage against defined criteria, so that the Company could demonstrate the performance of the work to the client, the party wall surveyor and the building control authority.

The advance was not a matter of commercial preference. It was a question of whether the Company's approach to deep basement construction could be extended into more demanding ground than it had previously worked in, and whether the result could be recorded in a form that would allow the Company to repeat it.

### 5.6 Project P1: Technological uncertainties

Three uncertainties were identified before the work began.

**Uncertainty 1: stability of the adjacent party wall during excavation.** The party wall carried the load of the adjoining house on a shallow foundation. Excavation to formation depth would reduce the lateral support available to the wall. The degree of movement that the wall could accommodate, and the point at which that movement would become unacceptable, could not be readily deduced from the desk study or from the design information available at the time.

**Uncertainty 2: unknown ground conditions.** The made ground beneath and around the excavation was of uncertain extent and character. Its stiffness, permeability and response to dewatering could not be confirmed until the piles were installed and the first excavation stage was taken down. The presence of obstructions, including old foundations and services, was also unknown.

**Uncertainty 3: sequencing of propping in a high water table.** The sequence of excavation and propping needed to control both ground movement and groundwater inflow. The number of propping levels, their timing and the extent of dewatering required were not known in advance and could not be determined by a standard method without monitoring data from the site.

Each uncertainty was identified in writing before the work started and was recorded in the design-development log, with a corresponding monitoring or investigation response.

### 5.7 Project P1: Systematic approach adopted

The Company adopted a staged approach, in which each design or temporary works decision was set out as a hypothesis, tested by measurement on site, and then either confirmed or revised. The approach was applied in the following way.

First, before piling began, the Company installed an array of inclinometers in the ground beside the party wall and a set of groundwater standpipes within the excavation footprint. Baseline readings were taken over a period of several weeks, so that the normal pattern of movement and groundwater level was known before any load was changed.

Second, the Company set amber and red monitoring triggers for lateral movement and for vertical movement of the party wall. The triggers were agreed with the client's design team and the party wall surveyor. The triggers were set so that an amber reading required the site team to review the temporary works before the next stage, and a red reading required the work to stop until the designer had confirmed the response.

Third, each excavation stage and each propping level was treated as an iteration. Designs and temporary works were iteratively developed, tested against defined success criteria, and refined based on observed performance. The success criteria were that movement of the party wall remained within the amber trigger, that groundwater inflow was controlled within the dewatering arrangement, and that each propping level was installed in the sequence set out in the method statement.

Fourth, every iteration was recorded in the project log, with the date, the readings, the decision taken and the person responsible. Site photographs were taken at each stage, and requests for information (RFIs) were raised whenever the design or the ground differed from the assumptions in the current method statement. The RFI register and the monitoring log are listed in the evidence index at Section 7.

Fifth, the Company reviewed the results of each stage before releasing the next. This review was carried out by the site engineering team with the Contracts Manager, and where the designer's input was needed, it was obtained before the next stage proceeded.

All activities were undertaken in a systematic, iterative and controlled manner, with outcomes recorded contemporaneously.

### 5.8 Project P1: Competent professional statement

The technical work on P1 was directed by Darren Kettlewell, a director of the Company with an NVQ Level 6 qualification in Construction Site Management and some twenty years' experience in groundworks, including the supervision of excavation, dewatering and temporary works on urban sites. He is not a chartered engineer. His knowledge of the practice of groundwork and basement construction, and his experience of the response of party walls and made ground to staged excavation, meant that he was able to judge the significance of the monitoring results and of the proposed changes to the sequence. Where the judgement required structural design input, that input was obtained from the designer before the relevant work proceeded.

The competent professional for the purposes of P1 therefore had the knowledge, experience and judgement of an experienced practitioner in the Company's field, and was able to determine what was known, what was not known, and how the unknowns could be resolved on site.
