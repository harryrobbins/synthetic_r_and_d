## 4. Company Background

### 4.1 History and purpose

Quillon Hydrogen Ltd was incorporated in 2021 by a small group of engineers who had worked together on fuel cell and electrolyser programmes in the North East of England and in continental Europe. The founders identified that the cost of green hydrogen is governed to a large extent by the cost of electrolyser stacks, and that the stack cost is in turn dominated by the platinum-group metal content of the catalyst layers. The Company was formed to reduce that content without sacrificing the durability that customers require.

The Company designs and manufactures PEM electrolyser stacks in modules of 250 kW and 1 MW. It sells its stacks to developers of hydrogen production facilities and to industrial users, and it has supplied paid pilot contracts to three customers during the period. The Company does not yet have a fully commercial product line, and its revenue is currently derived from pilot contracts and engineering services. Its turnover for the period was £380,000.

### 4.2 Ownership and group structure

Quillon Hydrogen Ltd is a private limited company and is not part of a group. The Company has no subsidiaries and no overseas branches. Its ordinary shares are held by the founders, by employee share option holders and by its Series A investors. The Company completed a Series A equity round of £14 million in 2024, led by a UK climate-focused venture investor, which provides the Company with sufficient funding to continue its development programme for the foreseeable future.

The board comprises the Chief Executive Officer, the Chief Technology Officer, the Commercial Director, two non-executive directors appointed by the Series A investors and one independent non-executive director with a background in energy infrastructure.

### 4.3 Funding

The Company's principal sources of funding during the period were:

- equity raised in the Series A round, drawn down during 2024 and 2025 against the Company's development milestones;
- a grant of £450,000 from Innovate UK under the LOWIR programme, which is applied to QH-LOWIR and is disclosed in the Additional Information Form;
- income from paid pilot contracts, which is shown in the Company's accounts and is modest in relation to its development costs.

The Company has not sought or received any state aid or subsidy other than the Innovate UK grant. The grant was awarded on a competitive basis for the development of low-iridium catalyst layers and was not awarded in connection with any overseas activity.

### 4.4 Staff

At the end of the period the Company employed 23 people. The R&D team comprises the following individuals, whose roles are described in the cost schedule at section 6:

- Dr Nadia Fenwick, Chief Technology Officer, who is the competent professional for this claim. She holds a PhD in electrochemistry and has twelve years' experience in fuel cells and electrolysers, including research leadership at a German stack manufacturer.
- Dr Joel Abernethy, Electrochemist, who leads the electrochemical characterisation of MEAs and the interpretation of polarisation and impedance data.
- Dr Sun-Hee Park, Materials Scientist, who is responsible for ionomer formulation and the interaction between ionomer and catalyst at low loading.
- Felix Morgan and Ruth Akinola, Test Engineers, who build and operate the single-cell and stack test rigs and who run the test protocols on each MEA build.

Harriet Lowe, Chief Executive Officer, and Owen Bryce, Commercial Director, are also included in the claim in respect of the proportions of their time described in section 6. The Company's other employees are engaged in manufacturing, sales, finance and administration and are not included in the claim.

### 4.5 Facilities

The Company's headquarters is at Building 4, Tyneford Energy Park, where it occupies laboratory, office and light assembly space. The laboratory contains a glovebox for catalyst ink preparation, a hot-press for MEA fabrication, two single-cell test stations rated to 100 A, a potentiostat for electrochemical characterisation, and a scanning electron microscope with energy dispersive X-ray capability, which is accessed under a service arrangement.

The Company's stack test facility at Tyneford comprises three stack test benches and a dynamic load emulator capable of reproducing the current profiles of renewable supply. The load emulator was commissioned in February 2025 and has been used on both projects since that date.

The 250 kW demonstrator pilot plant is installed at the Seal Sands East site under a site licence. The pilot plant is used to operate stacks under production-like conditions and to supply hydrogen to two pilot customers. Its operating costs are a commercial matter for the Company and are not part of the R&D claim, since the pilot plant is operated for demonstration of a commercial product.

### 4.6 Premises and equipment

The Company's equipment is recorded in its fixed asset register and depreciated over its useful life. The Company has claimed no capital allowances in respect of the R&D equipment in this period. Materials, consumables and external testing services are recorded in the Company's purchase ledger and reconciled to the claim in section 6.

### 4.7 Relationships with other organisations

The Company works with the University of Tyneford, which provides X-ray photoelectron spectroscopy (XPS) analysis of catalyst surfaces under a fee-paying arrangement. The University has no interest in the Company and is not connected to it in any other way. The Company also works with an accelerated stress testing laboratory in the Netherlands, described in section 5, which is operated by an independent foundation.

The Company is a member of a regional hydrogen cluster and has taken part in industry working groups on electrolyser durability standards. These memberships have not generated any income for the Company and are not claimed.

### 4.8 Growth and headcount

The Company grew from eleven employees at the start of 2024 to 23 at the end of 2025. Most of the growth has been in the R&D team and in pilot plant operations. The Company expects to recruit a further six staff in 2026, principally in test engineering and in manufacturing.
