### 5.11 Chronological work log, QH-LOWIR: second half of 2025

The second half of the period was dominated by the coating redesign and by the long-duration test that established durability at the target loading.

#### 5.11.1 July 2025

The Nb-doped coating was applied to a first set of PTLs on 2 July, and MEA-026 and MEA-027 were built with the coating on 8 and 15 July. The two builds were tested under the emulator profile. MEA-026 showed delamination of the coating at the outer step after 310 hours, visible under the scanning electron microscope as a lifting of the coating from the titanium substrate. MEA-027 showed no delamination but a higher contact resistance than the earlier builds, which was attributed to the coating thickness (QHL-LB-0488, QHL-TD-0491).

The delamination was attributed to the coating process, which had been developed at a smaller scale and had not been adjusted for the larger PTL area used in the MEA builds. The process was revised on 18 July, and the revised coating was used in all subsequent builds (QHL-JR-2281).

On 21 July the Company dispatched a set of coated PTLs and matched catalyst-coated membranes to the accelerated stress testing laboratory in the Netherlands. The dispatch and the protocol are described in section 5.12.

#### 5.11.2 August 2025

MEA-028 and MEA-029 were built on 4 and 11 August with the revised coating process. Both were tested under the emulator profile. MEA-028 showed no delamination after 500 hours, and its degradation rate over that period was 4.9 µV/h at a loading of 0.21 mg/cm². This was the first build at a loading near the target to show degradation below 5 µV/h. MEA-029 was built with the same coating at a loading of 0.19 mg/cm² and showed a degradation rate of 6.6 µV/h over its first 300 hours (QHL-LB-0503, QHL-TD-0509).

The ionomer IF-4 was used in both builds without change. The graded layer was reduced to a three-step structure with a lower loading at the outer step, which the Company judged would reduce the local current density at the PTL interface and therefore the risk of passivation.

On 19 August the Company reviewed the position with its Board. The review concluded that the combination of the niobium-doped coating, IF-4 and the three-step layer was the best available candidate for the target, and that the next task was a long-duration test of a build at 0.18 mg/cm² under the full emulator profile (QHL-MIN-0819).

#### 5.11.3 September 2025

MEA-030 was built on 2 September at a loading of 0.18 mg/cm². It was intended as the candidate for the long-duration test, but it showed a rise in contact resistance after 150 hours. Post-test XPS analysis on 12 September showed a thin passive film at the outer step of the PTL. The film was thinner than the film found in May, but its presence showed that the coating thickness at the outer step was still insufficient for the local potential (QHL-AN-0915).

The coating was redesigned on 15 September, with a thicker niobium-doped film at the outer step and a tapered profile at the edge of the active area. The new coating was designated Nb-v3. MEA-031 and MEA-032 were built on 18 and 23 September with Nb-v3 and tested under the emulator profile. Neither showed a rise in contact resistance over 400 hours, and XPS analysis of MEA-032 on 29 September found no passive oxide at the outer step or elsewhere on the PTL interface (QHL-AN-0929).

The passivation failure is therefore regarded by the Company as resolved in its initial form. The Company continues to monitor the PTL interface in long-duration tests, as described in section 5.13.

#### 5.11.4 October 2025

MEA-033 was built on 25 September with Nb-v3 at a loading of 0.18 mg/cm². The long-duration test began on 28 September, and the build was tested under the full emulator profile at 2 A/cm². The test is the principal durability result of QH-LOWIR for the period. The protocol required a minimum of 2,000 hours of operation with degradation rate measured at 100-hour intervals, and with impedance spectroscopy and voltage logs stored continuously (QHL-TD-0928, QHL-LB-0931).

MEA-034 was built on 9 October at 0.18 mg/cm² on the same coating, with a modified ionomer loading intended to test the sensitivity of the result to the ionomer content. The build was tested from 14 October. Its initial degradation rate was higher than MEA-033 over the first 200 hours, which the Company attributed to the conditioning of the modified ionomer. The build was retained for the remainder of the period as a comparison (QHL-LB-0940).

The Company also reviewed the AST results from the Netherlands laboratory in October. The results are discussed in section 5.12.

#### 5.11.5 November 2025

MEA-035 was built on 6 November with a graded layer at 0.17 mg/cm², the lowest loading tested in the period, on the Nb-v3 coating. It was tested from 11 November. The early data showed degradation of 7.9 µV/h over the first 250 hours. This build was not taken to the full 2,000 hours, because the Company considered that the data were sufficient to set the loading range for the next stage of work and because the test rig was required for QH-DYNA from 17 November (QHL-LB-1106).

MEA-033 continued in the long-duration test through November. Its degradation rate, measured at the 100-hour intervals, was stable in the range 6.2 to 7.1 µV/h, with no sign of a rise in the high-frequency resistance. Post-test XPS was scheduled for the end of the test, since the protocol required the MEA to be taken off test before characterisation.

#### 5.11.6 December 2025

MEA-036 was built on 3 December as the final build of the period, using the Nb-v3 coating at 0.18 mg/cm² with the ionomer IF-4 and the three-step layer. The build was used to confirm the reproducibility of the MEA-033 result and was tested from 8 December (QHL-LB-1203).

MEA-033 reached 2,000 hours of operation on 20 December 2025. Its mean degradation rate over the test was 6.8 µV/h at 0.18 mg/cm² and 2 A/cm², which meets the Company's target of degradation below 10 µV/h at the loading below 0.2 mg/cm² set in the project plan. The result is recorded in the test report QHL-TD-1221 and is the principal outcome of QH-LOWIR for the period. The MEA was not taken off test at 2,000 hours, and the test continued into the following period, since the Company intends to establish the durability of the layer over the full target life of the stack.

The post-test XPS analysis of MEA-033, taken after the test was paused on 22 December, showed no passive oxide at the PTL interface and an iridium oxidation state consistent with the start-of-test state. The analysis is recorded in QHL-AN-1224.

### 5.12 Accelerated stress testing and the external laboratory

#### 5.12.1 Purpose of the AST campaign

The Company's long-duration tests take up to 2,000 hours to show a meaningful degradation trend, and the failure modes that matter for the target life of a stack, such as support dissolution and slow passivation, can take many thousands of hours to appear under normal operation. Accelerated stress testing is the standard means of compressing this timescale. The AST protocol applies repeated potential cycles, with high upper potentials, to the MEA. The conditions drive support dissolution and PTL passivation in a time-compressed manner, so that the Company can assess whether a candidate layer will survive the target life before committing to the full test.

The Company's AST protocol was developed in 2024 and was specific to the Company's cell geometry, the PTL area used in its MEAs and the iridium loadings targeted by QH-LOWIR. Its design was based on the published AST literature for low-loading anodes, adapted for the Company's layer structure and for the dynamic profile used in the emulator tests.

#### 5.12.2 Choice of laboratory

The AST campaign was carried out by Stichting Waterstof Testlab, an independent foundation based in the Netherlands (the "Dutch laboratory"). The Dutch laboratory was selected because it is the only facility able to run the Company's bespoke AST protocol at the required scale with immediate availability. The protocol requires a multi-cell test rig able to hold eight MEAs under potential cycling at the same time, with independent control of each cell and continuous logging of potential and current. The Company's enquiries in 2024 identified no UK facility with a rig of this specification that could take the work in the period. Two UK laboratories were approached. Both offered to build a comparable rig, but each estimated a lead time of around six months, which would have delayed the programme by six months and would have pushed the test of the coated PTL beyond the period in which the Company's pilot customers had scheduled their stack evaluations.

The Dutch laboratory offered immediate availability, a rig already configured for the Company's protocol with minimal adaptation, and a schedule that allowed the first campaign to begin in late July 2025. The Company's view, supported by its technical advisers, was that the value of the AST results depended on their timing as much as on their quality, and that the choice of the Dutch laboratory was the correct technical and commercial decision for the programme.

#### 5.12.3 AST campaign and results

The Company dispatched coated PTLs and matched catalyst-coated membranes to the Dutch laboratory on 21 July 2025. The first campaign ran from 28 July to 12 September 2025 and tested eight MEAs, comprising the Nb-doped coating in its first and second forms and the Nb-v3 coating. The campaign report, reference QHL-AST-2025-01, is dated 26 September 2025 and is included in the evidence index.

The campaign showed that the first and second forms of the coating were not sufficient for the outer step, consistent with the in-house results described in section 5.11.3. The Nb-v3 coating survived the AST protocol with no delamination and no passive oxide formation at the outer step, which supported the decision to take Nb-v3 forward into long-duration testing. A second campaign on the final loading range began on 3 November and is reported in QHL-AST-2025-02, which was received on 15 December 2025.

The laboratory's services were invoiced at £120,000 for the campaigns in the period, reference INV-SWT-2025-0031 and INV-SWT-2025-0047. The Company's cost claim for the laboratory is set out in section 6.

#### 5.12.4 Relationship to the claim

The AST work was a direct part of the investigation of the failure modes of QH-LOWIR, and was carried out under the direction of Dr Nadia Fenwick and Dr Joel Abernethy. The Company's staff specified each protocol, assessed each result and decided the design of each subsequent build. The Dutch laboratory provided the test environment and the raw data, and the Company's staff performed the interpretation.

### 5.13 Outcomes of QH-LOWIR

The principal outcomes of QH-LOWIR for the period are as follows.

First, the Company has demonstrated an anode iridium loading of 0.18 mg/cm² in a MEA operating at 2 A/cm² under the full emulator profile, with a mean degradation rate of 6.8 µV/h over 2,000 hours. This meets the combined loading and durability target that no published approach had achieved under dynamic load at the start of the period, as recorded in section 5.3.

Second, the Company has identified and resolved the passivation failure of the titanium PTL at the outer step. The failure mode was not anticipated in the project plan, and its resolution required the redesign of the coating, the development of an AST protocol and the adjustment of the layer structure. The resolution was verified by post-test XPS on MEA-032 and MEA-033.

Third, the Company has established the effect of the ionomer on adhesion and proton conductivity at low loading. The reformulation IF-4 was shown to resolve the adhesion loss found in February and March, and the sensitivity of the result to ionomer content is recorded in MEA-034.

Fourth, the Company has established that the coating process must be controlled at the scale of the production PTL, as shown by the delamination in July. This result has been transferred to the Company's manufacturing team.

The Company does not consider that QH-LOWIR is complete. The uncertainties recorded in section 5.5 are not fully resolved. The dissolution mechanism under the full renewable load profile has been characterised only over the period of the 2,000-hour test. The durability of the coated PTL beyond 2,000 hours has not yet been established, and the target life of the stack, which the Company's pilot customers specify at 20,000 hours, has not been reached by any build in the programme. The test of MEA-033 continues into the following period, and the work described in this section will be continued under the same project.
