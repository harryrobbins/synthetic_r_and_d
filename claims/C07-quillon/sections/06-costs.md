### 6.5 Methodology for apportionment

#### 6.5.1 Staff time

The Company operates a weekly timesheet system. Each employee records hours against project codes, with separate codes for QH-LOWIR, QH-DYNA, and the non-R&D activities of the business. The timesheets are approved weekly by the employee's line manager and reviewed each month by the finance function. The R&D percentage for each individual is the ratio of hours recorded against the two R&D project codes to total recorded hours for the period.

Hours for the R&D team were recorded in Jira, which is used by the Company for the planning and tracking of test campaigns and design iterations. Each MEA build, test campaign and coolant plan has a Jira ticket, and each ticket is linked to a timesheet code. The Company's finance function reconciled the timesheet records against the Jira ticket history for the R&D team in January 2026. The reconciliation identified minor differences in the recording of time for the test rig commissioning, which were corrected in the timesheets before the figures were used in this claim.

For those individuals whose time is not fully recorded in Jira, including the Chief Executive Officer and the Commercial Director, the percentage was established by interview with the individual and with the Chief Technology Officer, and confirmed against the calendar and email records for the period. The interviews were conducted in November 2025 and the notes are held on the claim file.

#### 6.5.2 Chief Executive Officer

The Chief Executive Officer's time on the R&D projects consists principally of attendance at technical reviews, review of the test data for each project, and approval of the technical plan at each stage. Interview with the Chief Executive Officer established that she attended the QH-LOWIR and QH-DYNA technical reviews on a fortnightly basis, and that the time spent on each review, together with the associated preparation and follow-up, averaged around 15% of her recorded working time over the period. This is the basis of the 15% in the staff table.

#### 6.5.3 Commercial Director

The Commercial Director's R&D time is attributed to the technical customer requirement gathering that informed both projects. The Commercial Director took part in customer meetings with the Company's pilot customers, recorded the performance and durability requirements that the customers stated, and passed those requirements to the R&D team. This work defined the targets for QH-LOWIR and the operating profile for QH-DYNA. Interview with the Commercial Director and with the Chief Technology Officer established that approximately 10% of his working time was spent in this activity over the period. The percentage was confirmed against the customer meeting records and the requirements log, reference QHL-CRQ-2025.

#### 6.5.4 Materials and consumables

Consumables comprise membranes, catalyst-coated substrates, titanium porous transport layer material and iridium. Consumables were purchased for the MEA builds and the stack tests of both projects. The whole of the consumables expenditure is claimed, since it was purchased for and used in the R&D activity, and none was used in the pilot plant operation. The purchase records are reconciled to the claim in the supplier list at Appendix 7.4.

#### 6.5.5 Subcontracted testing

The Company's accelerated stress testing was carried out by Stichting Waterstof Testlab, in the Netherlands, under the invoices listed at Appendix 7.4. The invoices for the period total £120,000. The Company has claimed 65% of this amount, being the proportion of the laboratory's work that the Company's engineers have assessed as relating to the Company's R&D tests, as opposed to the laboratory's standard reporting and calibration work. The assessment was made from the laboratory's statements of work and was reviewed by the Chief Technology Officer.

The XPS surface analysis was carried out by the University of Tyneford, a UK higher education institution, under a fee-paying arrangement described in section 4.7. The invoices total £22,000, and 65% of this amount has been claimed on the same basis, namely the proportion of the University's analysis that relates to the Company's R&D samples.

The Company's view is that subcontracted testing is qualifying expenditure as the work was carried out for the purpose of the R&D projects, and that the laboratory's services were an essential part of the investigation of the failure modes described in section 5.

#### 6.5.6 Software and data

The COMSOL-style multiphysics licence is used for the electrochemical and thermal modelling of both projects. The full licence cost of £18,000 is claimed. The Company's view is that the licence is used for the R&D modelling and that no other use of the licence is made in the business.

Test-rig telemetry storage holds the data generated by the single-cell and stack test rigs, including the dynamic load emulator data used for both projects. The storage cost of £4,200 is claimed in full on the basis that the storage is used for the R&D data.

### 6.6 Intensity calculation

The intensity of the claim is the proportion of the Company's total relevant expenditure for the period that is qualifying R&D expenditure. The calculation is set out below.

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Qualifying R&D expenditure (section 6.4) | £715,519 |
| Total relevant expenditure (see note 1) | £2,250,000 |
| **Intensity as claimed** | **31.8%** |

Note 1: The total relevant expenditure is the Company's total expenditure for the period, as recorded in its management accounts. Pilot plant operating costs (£420,000) are excluded from total expenditure as they relate to a separate demonstration activity, which the Company operates at the Seal Sands East site for the purpose of demonstrating a commercial product to pilot customers. The demonstration activity is not an R&D project and its costs are not claimed.

### 6.7 Relief computation

The relief claimed is computed on the qualifying expenditure at section 6.4 using the rates for the enhanced expenditure scheme, which the Company has applied to the accounting period. The computation is set out below.

- extra deduction: £615,346
- surrenderable loss: £1,330,865
- payable credit: £192,975
- company turnover for context: £380,000
- total relevant expenditure (claimed, for intensity): £2,250,000; intensity as claimed = 31.8%
- trading loss before R&D deduction: £1,900,000

Relief computation (as claimed) – 86% additional deduction; 14.5% payable credit on surrendered loss.

The extra deduction is 86% of the qualifying expenditure. The surrenderable loss is the qualifying expenditure with the additional deduction applied, and the payable credit is 14.5% of the surrenderable loss.

### 6.8 Grant

The grant of £450,000 from Innovate UK under the LOWIR programme relates to QH-LOWIR. The Company has disclosed the grant in full in the Additional Information Form. The grant is not deducted from the qualifying expenditure in section 6.4. The Company's view is that the grant does not restrict the claim under the rules that apply to R&D claims made in respect of expenditure incurred on or after 1 April 2024.

### 6.9 Reconciliation to the accounts

The qualifying expenditure is reconciled to the Company's management accounts for the period. The staff costs reconcile to the payroll ledger after the R&D percentages are applied. The other qualifying expenditure reconciles to the purchase ledger and to the invoices listed at Appendix 7.4. The total relevant expenditure of £2,250,000 is taken from the management accounts for the period, as described in note 1 above.

The Company's finance function prepared the reconciliation and the Chief Executive Officer has reviewed it. Any difference between the schedules and the ledgers is recorded in the reconciliation file, which is held on the claim file.
