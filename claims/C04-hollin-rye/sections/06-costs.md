## 6. Costs

### 6.1 Basis of preparation

This section sets out the qualifying expenditure claimed for the accounting period from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025, the methodology used to apportion costs to the three projects, and the computation of the expenditure credit. All figures are in pounds sterling. Employer National Insurance Contributions are calculated on the basis of the 2024-25 rates, and employer pension contributions are taken from the payroll records for the period.

The qualifying expenditure is split into three categories: staff costs, consumables and subcontracted work. Staff costs are the largest category, and the apportionment for each individual is explained at section 6.2. Consumables are explained at section 6.3 and subcontracted work at section 6.4. The relief computation is at section 6.5.

The Company's payroll records provide the salary, employer National Insurance and employer pension figures for each individual. The apportionment percentages are derived from the sources described in section 6.2, and each percentage has been reviewed against the trial schedules and the project records described in section 5.

#### 6.2.2 Apportionment of staff time

**Marcus Rye, Executive Chef and Director (60%).** Mr Rye's time was apportioned using his working rota for the period, combined with the trial schedules and tasting records for each menu cycle. The executive chef led the design, tasting and assessment of every fermentation and sous-vide trial, and his attendance at trials is recorded in the trial book. His remaining time was spent on the three restaurant sites, on supplier relationships, on the recruitment and training of kitchen staff, and on the day-to-day running of the Leeds kitchen. The 60% figure was agreed with the Managing Director in the course of the claim preparation and is supported by an estimate, reviewed against the trial book entries for each month, that Mr Rye spent approximately three days in every five on trial and development work across the period.

**Isla Hollin, Managing Director (30%).** Ms Hollin's time was apportioned on the basis of an interview with the agent, supported by her calendar for the period and the minutes of the Company's monthly management meetings. Ms Hollin provided strategic direction for the R&D programme, approved the trial budgets, reviewed the menu development outputs and set the commercial parameters for each menu cycle, including the food cost margins within which the tasting menu was to be priced. The percentage reflects her time in these activities, which the Company regards as the strategic direction of the R&D programme. Her remaining time was spent on finance, property, operations and the day-to-day management of the three sites.

**Tobias Wren, Head Chef, Leeds (50%), and Carmen Ruiz, Head Chef, York (50%).** The head chefs' time was apportioned on the basis of timesheets kept for the period. Each head chef recorded time spent on trial batches, tasting sessions and development meetings against the project codes used for the claim. The timesheets show that each head chef spent around half of their working time on trial work across the period, with the proportion rising in the run-up to each menu launch and falling in the weeks following launch. The octopus trials for project P2 were led by Ms Ruiz at York, and the short-rib trials by Mr Wren at Leeds.

**Kitchen brigade, 14 FTE (25%).** The kitchen brigade comprises the sous and line chefs at the three sites. Their time is apportioned at 25% on the basis that each member of the brigade participated in the menu trials during the period. The trial rota for each menu cycle assigned members of the brigade to tasting sessions, batch preparation and trial service, and the Company estimates that around a quarter of the brigade's paid hours were spent on this work. The estimate was made by the executive chef from the trial rota and confirmed against the brigade's hours records for a sample of four weeks in each quarter. The brigade's remaining time was spent on menu service, prep, ordering, cleaning and the production of the restaurant menus.

**Ella Brandt, Food Technologist (80%).** Ms Brandt's time was apportioned on the basis of her timesheets, which record her time against the H&R At Home shelf-life programme, the packaging trials at the Leeds commissary, the challenge-testing programme with Fenbridge Food Labs and the associated documentation. The proportion reflects the share of her working week spent on the shelf-life programme and associated trial work across the period. Her remaining time was spent on supplier specifications, food safety documentation, labelling and the technical support of the restaurant sites.

### 6.3 Consumables

#### 6.3.1 Ingredients for menu development (£64,000)

The Company has claimed £64,000 for ingredients consumed in menu development. The figure is taken from the Company's purchase records for the period, using the supplier invoices listed at section 7.5. It comprises the raw materials used in the fermentation, sous-vide and sensory trials under projects P1 and P2, including rice koji, cured and fermented fish and vegetables, proteins used in the octopus and short-rib trials, and the other materials used in the development kitchen.

The Company's view is that the ingredients used in the development of the fermentation-led flavour systems and sous-vide regimes form part of the qualifying expenditure in full. The dishes developed through the trial process were introduced to the tasting menu on the launch dates recorded at section 5.2.6 and at section 5.3.6, and the ingredients used in their development are therefore consumed in the R&D process itself. The Company has not made any deduction for the portion of ingredients that went into dishes subsequently served on the menus, as the development of each dish is part of the same process of resolving the flavour and texture uncertainties described in section 5.

The Company has adopted the purchase records as the basis for the figure. Ingredient costs were recorded by invoice and allocated to the development kitchen using the trial book. Ingredient costs for the restaurant sites in general service are excluded.

#### 6.3.2 Sous-vide and fermentation equipment (£22,500)

The Company has claimed £22,500 for sous-vide and fermentation equipment. This comprises three circulating water baths, a chamber vacuum sealer and a fermentation chamber, purchased under purchase orders PO-0624-11 and PO-0624-12 in June 2024 and brought into use in July 2024.

The Company treats this equipment as consumable for the purposes of the claim. The items are used exclusively in the trial programmes described in section 5, and their working life is tied to the duration of the R&D programme and the number of trial batches processed. The Company does not expect the equipment to be used in its restaurant operations once the programmes are complete, and it has therefore treated the full cost as a consumable item in the period.

### 6.4 Subcontracted work

The Company has claimed £7,800 for subcontracted microbiology work carried out by Fenbridge Food Labs, a UK accredited laboratory that is unconnected with the Company. The total fee for the challenge-testing programme was £12,000. The Company has apportioned 65% of that fee to the shelf-life programme under project P3, reflecting the share of the laboratory's test schedule that related to the shelf-life protocol rather than to the Company's other food safety testing for the period.

The subcontract was carried out under a written protocol agreed on 12 September 2024. The laboratory's invoice is held at HR-EV-058 and the laboratory reports for the three challenge runs at HR-EV-046, HR-EV-050 and HR-EV-052.

#### 6.5.2 Relief computation

The Company is claiming under the merged scheme, having been in profit throughout the period. The relief is calculated as an expenditure credit at 20% of qualifying expenditure, which is taxable as income of the Company.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Total qualifying expenditure | £379,051 |
| Expenditure credit at 20% | £75,810 |
| Net benefit after corporation tax at 25% (75% of expenditure credit) | £56,858 |
| Company turnover, accounting period | £6,200,000 |

The net benefit represents approximately 15% of qualifying expenditure, after tax on the expenditure credit at the main rate of corporation tax of 25%. The Company's turnover for context is £6,200,000 and the claim is therefore made in respect of a small or medium-sized enterprise.

The Company has confirmed that no part of the expenditure claimed has been, or will be, claimed under any other relief or grant.
