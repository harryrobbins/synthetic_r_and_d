### 5.3 Project P2: Advanced sous-vide protein texturisation programme

#### 5.3.1 Project summary

Project P2 concerned the time and temperature regimes used to texturise two protein cuts, short rib of beef and octopus, for service on the tasting menu and the à la carte menu. The programme ran from 8 July 2024 to 31 January 2025 in the Leeds development kitchen, and was carried out alongside project P1 by the same kitchen brigade under the direction of the executive chef.

The sous-vide method involves sealing food in a vacuum pack and cooking it in a water bath at a precisely controlled temperature. The method is well established in professional kitchens. The Company's work was concerned not with the method in general, but with establishing how it could be applied to two specific cuts, at the volumes and holding times required by the Company's three sites, to produce a predictable texture on each service.

The programme was led by Marcus Rye, Executive Chef and Director. Carmen Ruiz, head chef at York, undertook the octopus trials, and Tobias Wren, head chef at Leeds, undertook the short-rib trials.

#### 5.3.2 Technological Advance Sought

The Company sought to establish a time and temperature standard for each of two cuts that would deliver a predictable, restaurant-quality texture at service scale. For octopus, this required a regime that would produce a consistent tenderness across large and small pieces and across batches. For short rib, it required a regime that would hold its texture through the hold periods between cooking and plating that the Company's service pattern requires.

The Company also sought to set out the equipment, pack sizes and handling steps needed to apply each regime in three kitchens with differing equipment. This was new to the Company, which had previously relied on individual chefs' experience with braising and roasting.

#### 5.3.3 Baseline

The baseline for project P2 is conventional braising and roasting of short rib and octopus in the Company's kitchens, supported by published sous-vide cookery guidance for commercial kitchens. Before the programme started, short rib was braised over a period of many hours in a traditional way and held in the braising liquor, while octopus was simmered and then grilled. Texture was controlled by the individual chef's experience. No recorded time and temperature standard existed for either cut.

#### 5.3.4 Technological Uncertainties

The first uncertainty was whether octopus could be textured to a consistent tenderness at service scale. Octopus has a high collagen content and a tendency to become either tough or mushy depending on the time and temperature used. The Company did not know whether a sous-vide regime could deliver a consistent result across the range of portion sizes used on the menu, or whether a pre-tenderising step would be needed.

The second uncertainty was whether a short-rib regime could hold its texture through the hold periods required by the service pattern. Short rib cooked sous-vide can become soft or lose its structure if held for too long at serving temperature. The Company needed to establish the maximum hold period compatible with its service requirements.

A third uncertainty, which is more commercial in nature, was whether the resulting dishes would be commercially viable at the Company's food cost margins, given the additional time and equipment required for the sous-vide process.

#### 5.3.5 Systematic Approach Adopted

Trials were carried out in a documented sequence. For each cut, a set of temperature and time combinations was selected based on published guidance and the executive chef's experience. Each combination was run on a defined sample, sealed in a vacuum pack, and cooked in a water bath with the temperature monitored by a calibrated probe. After cooking, each sample was assessed for texture, moisture, colour and flavour, using a scoring sheet completed by at least two members of the kitchen brigade.

Where a regime produced an acceptable result, the next trials varied the hold time and the portion size to establish the operating window. The results were recorded in the trial book against each batch, and the regimes were tested again on a fresh batch before being adopted as a working standard.

#### 5.3.6 Work carried out and chronological log

**8 July 2024.** The programme was formally opened. The water baths and chamber vacuum sealer were installed in the development kitchen following purchase order PO-0624-11 (HR-EV-008), and a short training session was held for the kitchen brigade on vacuum packing and probe use.

**11 July 2024.** The first short-rib trial, SV-S-01, was carried out at 58 degrees Celsius for 48 hours and compared with a second sample at 62 degrees Celsius for 24 hours. The 58-degree sample was judged to be too soft for the intended service format, while the 62-degree sample had a firmer structure and a more satisfactory bite. (HR-EV-009)

**19 July 2024.** The fermentation chamber purchased under PO-0624-12 was commissioned and brought into use for the koji trials in project P1. Its installation record is held at HR-EV-011.

**22 August 2024.** The first octopus trial, SV-O-01, was carried out at 72 degrees Celsius for six hours. The result was a tender but inconsistent texture, with the thicker tentacles remaining firmer than the thinner sections. A second trial at 78 degrees Celsius for four hours produced a more consistent result but a noticeably dry finish. (HR-EV-010)

**September to October 2024.** A series of octopus trials was carried out to explore portion size and the effect of a pre-tenderising step. The pre-tenderising step involved a short brine and a controlled rest before vacuum packing. The results were recorded in HR-LB-0340 to HR-LB-0349.

**19 November 2024.** Octopus trial SV-O-04 combined pre-tenderising with a regime of 82 degrees Celsius for three hours, and was assessed against the flavour and texture specification. The result was judged to be consistent across portion sizes and was adopted as the working standard for the octopus course. (HR-LB-0355)

**14 January 2025.** Short-rib hold trial SV-S-05 measured texture change over holding periods of two, four and six hours at serving temperature. The result identified four hours as the maximum hold period compatible with the service format, beyond which the texture softened to an extent the executive chef judged unacceptable. (HR-LB-0368)

**31 January 2025.** The programme was closed. Both working standards were issued to the York and Harrogate sites, and the regimes were incorporated into the kitchen production sheets for the Spring 2025 menu.

#### 5.3.7 Outcome

The outcome of project P2 was two documented sous-vide regimes: one for octopus, using pre-tenderising and a fixed time and temperature combination, and one for short rib, with a defined maximum hold period. Both regimes were recorded in production sheets, and both were used on the Spring 2025 menu.

The Company's view is that the uncertainty around octopus texture was resolved in November 2024, and that the short-rib hold period was resolved in January 2025. The commercial viability question was resolved by costing the dishes against the Company's food cost margins and confirming that the sous-vide process was consistent with them.

#### 5.3.8 Boundaries of project P2

Project P2 started on 8 July 2024, with the installation of the water baths and vacuum sealer, and ended on 31 January 2025, when the working standards were issued. Work on the Spring 2025 menu production sheets after that date is not claimed.

### 5.4 Project P3: Shelf-life extension for chilled retail range

#### 5.4.1 Project summary

Project P3 concerned the extension of the shelf life of a chilled braised dish sold through the H&R At Home retail range, from five days to twelve days, while maintaining microbiological safety and the sensory profile of the product. The work was led by Ella Brandt, Food Technologist, and was carried out in partnership with Fenbridge Food Labs, an accredited external microbiology laboratory that is unconnected with the Company.

The product is a chilled braised shin of beef with root vegetables, sold in a 400 gram tray for reheating at home. It was launched in September 2024. Before the programme, its shelf life was five days at refrigeration temperature, based on the Company's own sensory and microbiological records from the Leeds commissary.

The programme was carried out in three stages: a baseline review and packaging trials in the commissary, a laboratory challenge-testing programme with Fenbridge Food Labs, and a final validation of the proposed shelf life. The project ran from 2 April 2024 to 28 March 2025.

#### 5.4.2 Technological Advance Sought

The Company sought to extend the chilled shelf life of the braised product to twelve days through a combination of two measures. The first was modified-atmosphere packaging, in which the headspace of the tray is replaced with a gas mixture that slows the growth of aerobic spoilage organisms. The second was a natural antimicrobial, cultured dextrose, which is a fermentate ingredient used to inhibit the growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* and other organisms in chilled ready-to-eat products.

The Company did not believe that either measure alone would deliver the required shelf life with the level of microbiological control needed. It sought to establish whether the two in combination, applied to a product of this composition and water activity, could deliver twelve days of chilled life without compromising safety or sensory quality.

#### 5.4.3 Baseline

The baseline is the five-day shelf life of the existing chilled braised dish, supported by the Company's in-house sensory and microbiological records. The product was packed in a standard tray with a film lid and held at refrigeration temperature. No modified-atmosphere packaging had been used for the product, and no antimicrobial ingredient had been included in the recipe. The shelf-life limit was set by the Company's food safety specification, which uses the microbiological and sensory indicators applied across its chilled range.

The Company's technical baseline therefore reflects established hurdle technology: refrigeration, standard packaging, and the control of water activity and pH through recipe design. The Company does not claim that refrigeration or standard packaging are themselves advances.

#### 5.4.4 Technological Uncertainties

The principal uncertainty was whether the combination of modified-atmosphere packaging and cultured dextrose would control the growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* and other spoilage organisms over a twelve-day chilled life under the storage conditions a consumer might reasonably apply. Because the product is ready to eat and is sold for home reheating, the Company was required to show that the level of control was adequate for the full period, including under reasonable abuse temperature conditions.

The second uncertainty was whether the product would retain an acceptable flavour and texture under the proposed packaging conditions. Modified-atmosphere packaging can alter the colour and aroma of cooked meat, and cultured dextrose can contribute a faint tang that may or may not be acceptable in a braised dish. Both effects had to be assessed against the sensory profile of the dish.

A third uncertainty was the optimal concentration of cultured dextrose. Too little would give insufficient control of *Listeria*, while too much would alter the flavour and might require declaration in a form the Company did not wish to use.

#### 5.4.5 Systematic Approach Adopted

The programme followed a documented protocol. Packaging trials were carried out first in the Leeds commissary, using the modified-atmosphere packaging machine. Gas mixtures were varied, and the residual oxygen in each tray was measured after packing. Sensory assessments were made at fixed intervals against a reference sample held at refrigeration temperature.

The challenge-testing programme was carried out in three runs under a written protocol agreed with Fenbridge Food Labs. Each run inoculated the product with *Listeria monocytogenes* at a defined level, packed it under the trial conditions, and held it at refrigeration temperature and at a higher abuse temperature. Samples were tested at intervals for *Listeria* counts, water activity and pH. Each run was documented in the Company's trial records and in the laboratory's reports.

The Company's own laboratory records are maintained by the food technologist in a dedicated trial book, with each page dated and signed. Pages from that book are referenced in this section as HR-LB-nnnn.

#### 5.4.6 Work carried out and chronological log

**2 April 2024.** The project was opened. The food technologist reviewed the five-day baseline and the records behind it, and confirmed the specification limits to be used in the trials. (HR-LB-0401)

**4 June 2024.** Packaging trials began in the Leeds commissary. Trays of the braised dish were packed under four gas mixtures, with varying carbon dioxide and nitrogen content, and residual oxygen was measured after sealing. The mixture adopted for further work was 60% carbon dioxide and 40% nitrogen, which reduced residual oxygen to below 0.5%. (HR-LB-0412; HR-EV-041)

**18 June 2024.** Sensory trials under modified-atmosphere packaging identified a loss of the fresh aroma of the braising liquor after nine days at refrigeration temperature. The Company judged this to be a constraint that a natural antimicrobial might address, and the search for a suitable ingredient began. (HR-LB-0419)

**12 September 2024.** The Company made contact with Fenbridge Food Labs and agreed a challenge-testing protocol covering three runs, the inoculation levels, the sampling intervals and the acceptance criteria. (HR-EV-044)

**3 October 2024.** Challenge test C1 was started. The product was packed under the modified-atmosphere mixture with no antimicrobial, inoculated with *Listeria monocytogenes* at approximately 2.0 log colony forming units per gram, and held at 4 degrees Celsius and at 8 degrees Celsius. This run was designed to measure the effect of packaging alone.

**28 October 2024.** The results of C1 were reviewed. Under packaging alone, *Listeria* counts rose by approximately 1.6 log over twelve days at 8 degrees Celsius, which the food technologist judged to be above the acceptance criteria. The result confirmed that packaging alone was insufficient and that an antimicrobial was needed. (HR-EV-046)

**2 December 2024.** Challenge test C2 was started, with cultured dextrose added to the recipe at 0.4% by weight, in combination with the modified-atmosphere mixture. The same inoculation level and sampling schedule were used.

**13 January 2025.** The sensory assessment of C2 was completed. The flavour of the braised dish was judged to be acceptable with the cultured dextrose at 0.4%, but a faint tang was noted at day 10 and beyond. The microbiological results are reported in section 5.4.7.

**20 January 2025.** The Company agreed with Fenbridge Food Labs a third run, C3, to confirm the result at a slightly lower cultured dextrose concentration and a revised water activity target. The lower concentration was intended to reduce the tang without losing control of *Listeria*.

**10 February 2025.** Challenge test C3 was started with cultured dextrose at 0.3% by weight, and the water activity of the braised sauce was adjusted through a small reduction in the cooking liquor. The product was sampled at day 0, day 5, day 9, day 12 and day 14.

**28 February 2025.** The results of C3 were received and reviewed. Details are set out in section 5.4.7 below. The final shelf-life position of twelve days at refrigeration temperature was confirmed on this date.

**28 March 2025.** The project was closed in the Company's records, and the revised specification was issued to the Leeds commissary for use from the next production run. (HR-EV-048)
