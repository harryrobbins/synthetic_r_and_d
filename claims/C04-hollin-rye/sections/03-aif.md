### 3.7 Project 1: Development of novel fermentation-led flavour systems for a seasonal tasting menu

**Main field of science or technology**

Food science and technology: fermentation, flavour chemistry and culinary process development.

**Baseline**

The baseline is the Company's established practice in traditional restaurant techniques, as applied to its seasonal menus before April 2024. Dishes were built from conventional stocks, braises, cures and pickles, with flavour balance adjusted by experienced chefs through tasting. Fermented ingredients were used only in limited, well-established forms.

**Advance sought**

The Company sought to advance the state of knowledge and capability in producing bold, umami-forward flavour systems from koji, garums and lacto-fermented vegetables, and in applying them repeatably across a multi-course seasonal tasting menu.

**Technological uncertainties**

The principal uncertainties were whether diners would accept bold umami-forward profiles at the Company's price point, and achieving consistent flavour across batches of fermented ingredients produced over several weeks.

**How and when the uncertainties were resolved**

The uncertainties were addressed through a structured series of trial batches between April 2024 and March 2025, with each batch assessed by the kitchen brigade and the competent professional against a written flavour specification. Consistency was resolved through controlled fermentation conditions and recorded batch parameters. The question of guest acceptance was resolved by evaluating feedback across four seasonal menu launches, the last of which was introduced on 3 March 2025.

### 3.8 Project 2: Advanced sous-vide protein texturisation programme

**Main field of science or technology**

Food science and technology: protein texture, thermal processing and sous-vide cookery.

**Baseline**

The baseline is conventional braising and roasting of short rib and octopus in the Company's kitchens, supported by published sous-vide cookery guidance for commercial kitchens. Texture was controlled by the chef's experience, with no recorded time and temperature standard for the Company's specific cuts.

**Advance sought**

The Company sought to establish time and temperature regimes that deliver a predictable, restaurant-quality texture in short rib and octopus, including at the volumes required across three sites.

**Technological uncertainties**

The uncertainties were whether octopus could be textured to a consistent tenderness at service scale, and whether a short-rib regime could hold its texture through the hold periods required by the service pattern. There was also uncertainty about whether the resulting dishes would be commercially viable at the Company's food cost margins.

**How and when the uncertainties were resolved**

Trials were conducted between 11 July 2024 and 14 January 2025, with each run recorded by temperature, time and texture assessment. Octopus was resolved by a pre-tenderising step combined with a revised regime, validated in November 2024. Short-rib hold periods were resolved in January 2025.

### 3.9 Project 3: Shelf-life extension for chilled retail range

**Main field of science or technology**

Food science and technology: food preservation, microbiology and packaging.

**Baseline**

The baseline is the Company's documented shelf life for its chilled braised dish, which was five days at refrigeration temperature, based on in-house sensory and microbiological records. Hurdle technology in the form of refrigeration and standard packaging was used. No modified-atmosphere packaging or natural antimicrobial had been applied to the product.

**Advance sought**

The Company sought to extend the shelf life of the chilled braised dish from five days to twelve days, while maintaining microbiological safety and the sensory profile of the dish, through a combination of modified-atmosphere packaging and a natural antimicrobial.

**Technological uncertainties**

The uncertainties were whether a combination of modified-atmosphere packaging and cultured dextrose would control the growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* and other spoilage organisms over a twelve-day chilled life, and whether the product would retain acceptable flavour and texture under the proposed packaging conditions.

**How and when the uncertainties were resolved**

Challenge testing was undertaken with an external accredited laboratory. Three runs were completed between October 2024 and February 2025. The third run confirmed that the product met the protocol limits for *Listeria monocytogenes* growth at twelve days under abuse temperature conditions, with water activity and pH held within the specified range. The final shelf-life position was confirmed on 28 February 2025.
