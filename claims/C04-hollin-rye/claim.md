### 3.7 Project 1: Development of novel fermentation-led flavour systems for a seasonal tasting menu

**Main field of science or technology**

Food science and technology: fermentation, flavour chemistry and culinary process development.

**Baseline**

The baseline is the Company's established practice in traditional restaurant techniques, as applied to its seasonal menus before April 2024. Dishes were built from conventional stocks, braises, cures and pickles, with flavour balance adjusted by experienced chefs through tasting. Fermented ingredients were used only in limited, well-established forms.

**Advance sought**

The Company sought to advance the state of knowledge and capability in producing bold, umami-forward flavour systems from koji, garums and lacto-fermented vegetables, and in applying them repeatably across a multi-course seasonal tasting menu.

**Technological uncertainties**

The principal uncertainties were whether diners would accept bold umami-forward profiles at the Company's price point, and achieving consistent flavour across batches of fermented ingredients produced over several weeks.

**How and when the uncertainties were resolved**

The uncertainties were addressed through a structured series of trial batches between April 2024 and March 2025, with each batch assessed by the kitchen brigade and the competent professional against a written flavour specification. Consistency was resolved through controlled fermentation conditions and recorded batch parameters. The question of guest acceptance was resolved by evaluating feedback across four seasonal menu launches, the last of which was introduced on 3 March 2025.

### 3.8 Project 2: Advanced sous-vide protein texturisation programme

**Main field of science or technology**

Food science and technology: protein texture, thermal processing and sous-vide cookery.

**Baseline**

The baseline is conventional braising and roasting of short rib and octopus in the Company's kitchens, supported by published sous-vide cookery guidance for commercial kitchens. Texture was controlled by the chef's experience, with no recorded time and temperature standard for the Company's specific cuts.

**Advance sought**

The Company sought to establish time and temperature regimes that deliver a predictable, restaurant-quality texture in short rib and octopus, including at the volumes required across three sites.

**Technological uncertainties**

The uncertainties were whether octopus could be textured to a consistent tenderness at service scale, and whether a short-rib regime could hold its texture through the hold periods required by the service pattern. There was also uncertainty about whether the resulting dishes would be commercially viable at the Company's food cost margins.

**How and when the uncertainties were resolved**

Trials were conducted between 11 July 2024 and 14 January 2025, with each run recorded by temperature, time and texture assessment. Octopus was resolved by a pre-tenderising step combined with a revised regime, validated in November 2024. Short-rib hold periods were resolved in January 2025.

### 3.9 Project 3: Shelf-life extension for chilled retail range

**Main field of science or technology**

Food science and technology: food preservation, microbiology and packaging.

**Baseline**

The baseline is the Company's documented shelf life for its chilled braised dish, which was five days at refrigeration temperature, based on in-house sensory and microbiological records. Hurdle technology in the form of refrigeration and standard packaging was used. No modified-atmosphere packaging or natural antimicrobial had been applied to the product.

**Advance sought**

The Company sought to extend the shelf life of the chilled braised dish from five days to twelve days, while maintaining microbiological safety and the sensory profile of the dish, through a combination of modified-atmosphere packaging and a natural antimicrobial.

**Technological uncertainties**

The uncertainties were whether a combination of modified-atmosphere packaging and cultured dextrose would control the growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* and other spoilage organisms over a twelve-day chilled life, and whether the product would retain acceptable flavour and texture under the proposed packaging conditions.

**How and when the uncertainties were resolved**

Challenge testing was undertaken with an external accredited laboratory. Three runs were completed between October 2024 and February 2025. The third run confirmed that the product met the protocol limits for *Listeria monocytogenes* growth at twelve days under abuse temperature conditions, with water activity and pH held within the specified range. The final shelf-life position was confirmed on 28 February 2025.

## 5. Technical Narrative

### 5.0 Introduction

Throughout the claim period the Company sought to achieve an appreciable improvement in the overall knowledge and capability in its field, resolving uncertainties that could not be readily deduced by a competent professional working in the field, in accordance with paragraphs 3, 6 and 13 of the DSIT Guidelines.

This section sets out the technical narrative for each of the three projects claimed. For each project it describes the baseline, the advance sought, the technological uncertainties, the systematic approach adopted, the work carried out in chronological order, the outcome, and the start and end boundaries of the project. A competent professional statement is given at section 5.5 and a note on the boundaries of the claim at section 5.6.

The narrative is supported by the evidence index at section 7.3. Each evidence reference is given in the form HR-EV-nnn for documents, and HR-LB-nnnn for laboratory and trial book pages. All dates are within the accounting period from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025.

### 5.1 Overview of the Company's R&D programme

The Company's R&D programme during the period was driven by the design of its seasonal tasting menu and by the introduction of a chilled retail range. The tasting menu is the Company's principal creative output and must meet two requirements at once. It must be distinctive enough to justify the Company's position as a leading modern British restaurant group, and it must be repeatable across three sites, each with different equipment and different staff.

Before April 2024 the Company's menus were developed through a traditional process. The executive chef set a seasonal theme, the head chefs proposed dishes, and the kitchen brigade tasted and refined them until they met a standard the executive chef was satisfied with. This process produced good results, but it did not allow the Company to predict how a new ingredient, process or flavour would behave across batches, or how diners would respond to flavour profiles that departed from familiar British cooking.

The programme therefore set out to develop a more systematic understanding of three areas: fermentation-led flavour systems (project P1), the controlled texturisation of proteins by sous-vide and related methods (project P2), and the extension of shelf life for a chilled product through combined hurdle technology (project P3).

### 5.2 Project P1: Development of novel fermentation-led flavour systems for a seasonal tasting menu

#### 5.2.1 Project summary

Project P1 concerned the development of flavour systems based on koji, garums and lacto-fermented vegetables, and their application to the seasonal tasting menu. The work was carried out in the Leeds development kitchen between 1 April 2024 and 31 March 2025 and covered four seasonal menu cycles: Summer 2024, Autumn 2024, Winter 2024 and Spring 2025.

Each menu cycle required the development of between six and nine new dishes, many of which relied on at least one fermented component. The fermented components were developed in advance of each menu, tested in the development kitchen, adjusted through repeated tasting, and then introduced to the tasting menu. The dishes served on each menu were sold to paying guests in the Company's restaurants.

The project was led by Marcus Rye, Executive Chef and Director, with Tobias Wren and Carmen Ruiz as head chefs, and the kitchen brigade undertaking the trial work.

#### 5.2.2 Technological Advance Sought

The Company sought to advance its capability in producing bold, umami-forward flavour systems from fermented ingredients, and to apply them in a repeatable way across a multi-course seasonal menu. In particular it sought to establish:

- a reliable method for producing koji-based seasonings and glazes with consistent enzymatic activity;
- a working method for garum-style fish sauces made from seasonal fish and vegetable trim, with defined salt levels and fermentation times;
- a set of lacto-fermentation parameters for vegetables that retain texture while producing consistent acidity and flavour; and
- an approach to combining these components within a single dish without the flavour balance being dominated by any one of them.

Each of these was new to the Company. The Company's chefs had encountered fermentation in previous roles, but there was no documented method, no recorded parameter set and no means of predicting how a new batch of fermented material would behave when combined with other seasonal produce.

#### 5.2.3 Baseline

The baseline for project P1 is the Company's established practice in traditional restaurant techniques. Before April 2024 the Company's flavour work relied on conventional stocks, braises, cures and pickles, with balance achieved by experienced chefs tasting and adjusting dishes. Fermented ingredients were used on a limited basis, principally in pickles and dressings, and were prepared using standard household methods.

#### 5.2.4 Technological Uncertainties

Project P1 gave rise to two principal technological uncertainties.

The first was whether diners would accept bold umami-forward profiles at the Company's price point. The Company's guests were used to refined, familiar British flavours, and the Company did not know whether koji-cured and garum-based dishes would be well received on a tasting menu priced at the level of the Company's flagship restaurant. This uncertainty was not one that could be resolved by chemistry or by the chefs' own judgement. It could only be resolved by serving the dishes to guests and measuring their response.

The second was achieving consistent flavour across batches. Fermentation is a biological process, and the same recipe can produce noticeably different results from one batch to the next depending on ambient temperature, humidity, the age of the starter and the condition of the raw material. The Company needed to understand which variables controlled flavour outcomes and how tightly each needed to be managed to deliver a consistent dish across the four to six weeks over which a batch of fermented material would be used.

#### 5.2.5 Systematic Approach Adopted

The Company adopted a structured approach to these uncertainties. A trial schedule was set at the start of each menu cycle, identifying the fermented components to be developed, the number of trial batches, the parameters to be varied and the criteria for assessment. The schedule is recorded at HR-EV-002.

Each trial batch was recorded in a trial book, with the date, starter source, raw material, salt or moisture content, ambient temperature, duration and outcome. Tasting notes were written by the executive chef and the head chefs against a written flavour specification with defined attributes: umami intensity, acidity, saltiness, texture and aroma. Where a batch departed from the specification, the variable responsible was identified and the next batch was adjusted accordingly.

Batches were tasted blind where practicable, and results were compared across the kitchen brigade to reduce the risk of a single view dominating the outcome. Adjustments were made in a fixed sequence, one variable at a time, so that the effect of each change could be attributed.

The approach also included a guest response element. Dishes that had passed the internal flavour specification were introduced in a limited form on the menu, and feedback from guests was recorded in a structured form by front-of-house staff and reviewed by the executive chef after each service.

The work log below records the principal dated iterations of project P1. Each iteration is recorded in the trial book or in the supporting documents listed.

**1 to 12 April 2024.** The trial schedule for the Summer 2024 menu was agreed by the executive chef and head chefs. **September 2024.** Development of the Autumn 2024 menu began. #### 5.2.7 Outcome

The outcome of project P1 was a documented set of fermentation-led flavour systems, with recorded parameters for koji-based seasonings and glazes, garum-style sauces, and lacto-fermented vegetables. The parameter sets allow a new batch of fermented material to be produced to a known specification, and the flavour balance of the resulting dish to be predicted in advance.

On the question of guest acceptance, the Company's view is that the uncertainty was resolved during the period. Across the four menu cycles, guest response to fermented courses was consistently positive in the restaurant feedback records, and the proportion of guests reporting a strong preference for the fermented courses rose across the period. The Company has therefore concluded that bold umami-forward profiles are acceptable to its guests at its price point when applied with appropriate balance.

The outcome for batch consistency was that a storage and fermentation protocol was established and applied to all three sites from July 2024. Variation between batches was reduced to a level within the flavour specification for all of the working standards adopted from the Autumn 2024 cycle onward.

#### 5.2.8 Boundaries of project P1

Project P1 started on 1 April 2024, when the trial schedule for the Summer 2024 menu was agreed, and ended on 31 March 2025, when the final trial summary was completed. The Company has not claimed any work on the Spring 2026 menu cycle or on any work undertaken after 31 March 2025.

### 5.3 Project P2: Advanced sous-vide protein texturisation programme

#### 5.3.1 Project summary

Project P2 concerned the time and temperature regimes used to texturise two protein cuts, short rib of beef and octopus, for service on the tasting menu and the à la carte menu. The programme ran from 8 July 2024 to 31 January 2025 in the Leeds development kitchen, and was carried out alongside project P1 by the same kitchen brigade under the direction of the executive chef.

The sous-vide method involves sealing food in a vacuum pack and cooking it in a water bath at a precisely controlled temperature. The method is well established in professional kitchens. The Company's work was concerned not with the method in general, but with establishing how it could be applied to two specific cuts, at the volumes and holding times required by the Company's three sites, to produce a predictable texture on each service.

The programme was led by Marcus Rye, Executive Chef and Director. Carmen Ruiz, head chef at York, undertook the octopus trials, and Tobias Wren, head chef at Leeds, undertook the short-rib trials.

#### 5.3.2 Technological Advance Sought

The Company sought to establish a time and temperature standard for each of two cuts that would deliver a predictable, restaurant-quality texture at service scale. For octopus, this required a regime that would produce a consistent tenderness across large and small pieces and across batches. For short rib, it required a regime that would hold its texture through the hold periods between cooking and plating that the Company's service pattern requires.

The Company also sought to set out the equipment, pack sizes and handling steps needed to apply each regime in three kitchens with differing equipment. This was new to the Company, which had previously relied on individual chefs' experience with braising and roasting.

#### 5.3.3 Baseline

The baseline for project P2 is conventional braising and roasting of short rib and octopus in the Company's kitchens, supported by published sous-vide cookery guidance for commercial kitchens. Before the programme started, short rib was braised over a period of many hours in a traditional way and held in the braising liquor, while octopus was simmered and then grilled. Texture was controlled by the individual chef's experience. No recorded time and temperature standard existed for either cut.

#### 5.3.4 Technological Uncertainties

The first uncertainty was whether octopus could be textured to a consistent tenderness at service scale. Octopus has a high collagen content and a tendency to become either tough or mushy depending on the time and temperature used. The Company did not know whether a sous-vide regime could deliver a consistent result across the range of portion sizes used on the menu, or whether a pre-tenderising step would be needed.

The second uncertainty was whether a short-rib regime could hold its texture through the hold periods required by the service pattern. Short rib cooked sous-vide can become soft or lose its structure if held for too long at serving temperature. The Company needed to establish the maximum hold period compatible with its service requirements.

A third uncertainty, which is more commercial in nature, was whether the resulting dishes would be commercially viable at the Company's food cost margins, given the additional time and equipment required for the sous-vide process.

#### 5.3.5 Systematic Approach Adopted

Trials were carried out in a documented sequence. For each cut, a set of temperature and time combinations was selected based on published guidance and the executive chef's experience. Each combination was run on a defined sample, sealed in a vacuum pack, and cooked in a water bath with the temperature monitored by a calibrated probe. After cooking, each sample was assessed for texture, moisture, colour and flavour, using a scoring sheet completed by at least two members of the kitchen brigade.

Where a regime produced an acceptable result, the next trials varied the hold time and the portion size to establish the operating window. The results were recorded in the trial book against each batch, and the regimes were tested again on a fresh batch before being adopted as a working standard.

### 5.4 Project P3: Shelf-life extension for chilled retail range

#### 5.4.1 Project summary

Project P3 concerned the extension of the shelf life of a chilled braised dish sold through the H&R At Home retail range, from five days to twelve days, while maintaining microbiological safety and the sensory profile of the product. The work was led by Ella Brandt, Food Technologist, and was carried out in partnership with Fenbridge Food Labs, an accredited external microbiology laboratory that is unconnected with the Company.

The product is a chilled braised shin of beef with root vegetables, sold in a 400 gram tray for reheating at home. It was launched in September 2024. Before the programme, its shelf life was five days at refrigeration temperature, based on the Company's own sensory and microbiological records from the Leeds commissary.

The programme was carried out in three stages: a baseline review and packaging trials in the commissary, a laboratory challenge-testing programme with Fenbridge Food Labs, and a final validation of the proposed shelf life. The project ran from 2 April 2024 to 28 March 2025.

#### 5.4.2 Technological Advance Sought

The Company sought to extend the chilled shelf life of the braised product to twelve days through a combination of two measures. The first was modified-atmosphere packaging, in which the headspace of the tray is replaced with a gas mixture that slows the growth of aerobic spoilage organisms. The second was a natural antimicrobial, cultured dextrose, which is a fermentate ingredient used to inhibit the growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* and other organisms in chilled ready-to-eat products.

The Company did not believe that either measure alone would deliver the required shelf life with the level of microbiological control needed. It sought to establish whether the two in combination, applied to a product of this composition and water activity, could deliver twelve days of chilled life without compromising safety or sensory quality.

#### 5.4.3 Baseline

The baseline is the five-day shelf life of the existing chilled braised dish, supported by the Company's in-house sensory and microbiological records. The product was packed in a standard tray with a film lid and held at refrigeration temperature. No modified-atmosphere packaging had been used for the product, and no antimicrobial ingredient had been included in the recipe. The shelf-life limit was set by the Company's food safety specification, which uses the microbiological and sensory indicators applied across its chilled range.

The Company's technical baseline therefore reflects established hurdle technology: refrigeration, standard packaging, and the control of water activity and pH through recipe design. The Company does not claim that refrigeration or standard packaging are themselves advances.

#### 5.4.4 Technological Uncertainties

The principal uncertainty was whether the combination of modified-atmosphere packaging and cultured dextrose would control the growth of *Listeria monocytogenes* and other spoilage organisms over a twelve-day chilled life under the storage conditions a consumer might reasonably apply. Because the product is ready to eat and is sold for home reheating, the Company was required to show that the level of control was adequate for the full period, including under reasonable abuse temperature conditions.

The second uncertainty was whether the product would retain an acceptable flavour and texture under the proposed packaging conditions. Modified-atmosphere packaging can alter the colour and aroma of cooked meat, and cultured dextrose can contribute a faint tang that may or may not be acceptable in a braised dish. Both effects had to be assessed against the sensory profile of the dish.

A third uncertainty was the optimal concentration of cultured dextrose. Too little would give insufficient control of *Listeria*, while too much would alter the flavour and might require declaration in a form the Company did not wish to use.

#### 5.4.5 Systematic Approach Adopted

The programme followed a documented protocol. Packaging trials were carried out first in the Leeds commissary, using the modified-atmosphere packaging machine. Gas mixtures were varied, and the residual oxygen in each tray was measured after packing. Sensory assessments were made at fixed intervals against a reference sample held at refrigeration temperature.

The challenge-testing programme was carried out in three runs under a written protocol agreed with Fenbridge Food Labs. Each run inoculated the product with *Listeria monocytogenes* at a defined level, packed it under the trial conditions, and held it at refrigeration temperature and at a higher abuse temperature. Samples were tested at intervals for *Listeria* counts, water activity and pH. Each run was documented in the Company's trial records and in the laboratory's reports.

The Company's own laboratory records are maintained by the food technologist in a dedicated trial book, with each page dated and signed. Pages from that book are referenced in this section as HR-LB-nnnn.

#### 5.4.6 Work carried out and chronological log

**2 April 2024.** The project was opened. The food technologist reviewed the five-day baseline and the records behind it, and confirmed the specification limits to be used in the trials. (HR-LB-0401)

**4 June 2024.** Packaging trials began in the Leeds commissary. Trays of the braised dish were packed under four gas mixtures, with varying carbon dioxide and nitrogen content, and residual oxygen was measured after sealing. The mixture adopted for further work was 60% carbon dioxide and 40% nitrogen, which reduced residual oxygen to below 0.5%. (HR-LB-0412; HR-EV-041)

**18 June 2024.** Sensory trials under modified-atmosphere packaging identified a loss of the fresh aroma of the braising liquor after nine days at refrigeration temperature. The Company judged this to be a constraint that a natural antimicrobial might address, and the search for a suitable ingredient began. (HR-LB-0419)

**12 September 2024.** The Company made contact with Fenbridge Food Labs and agreed a challenge-testing protocol covering three runs, the inoculation levels, the sampling intervals and the acceptance criteria. (HR-EV-044)

**3 October 2024.** Challenge test C1 was started. The product was packed under the modified-atmosphere mixture with no antimicrobial, inoculated with *Listeria monocytogenes* at approximately 2.0 log colony forming units per gram, and held at 4 degrees Celsius and at 8 degrees Celsius. This run was designed to measure the effect of packaging alone.

**28 October 2024.** The results of C1 were reviewed. Under packaging alone, *Listeria* counts rose by approximately 1.6 log over twelve days at 8 degrees Celsius, which the food technologist judged to be above the acceptance criteria. The result confirmed that packaging alone was insufficient and that an antimicrobial was needed. (HR-EV-046)

**2 December 2024.** Challenge test C2 was started, with cultured dextrose added to the recipe at 0.4% by weight, in combination with the modified-atmosphere mixture. The same inoculation level and sampling schedule were used.

**13 January 2025.** The sensory assessment of C2 was completed. The flavour of the braised dish was judged to be acceptable with the cultured dextrose at 0.4%, but a faint tang was noted at day 10 and beyond. The microbiological results are reported in section 5.4.7.

**20 January 2025.** The Company agreed with Fenbridge Food Labs a third run, C3, to confirm the result at a slightly lower cultured dextrose concentration and a revised water activity target. The lower concentration was intended to reduce the tang without losing control of *Listeria*.

**10 February 2025.** Challenge test C3 was started with cultured dextrose at 0.3% by weight, and the water activity of the braised sauce was adjusted through a small reduction in the cooking liquor. The product was sampled at day 0, day 5, day 9, day 12 and day 14.

**28 February 2025.** The results of C3 were received and reviewed. Details are set out in section 5.4.7 below. The final shelf-life position of twelve days at refrigeration temperature was confirmed on this date.

**28 March 2025.** The project was closed in the Company's records, and the revised specification was issued to the Leeds commissary for use from the next production run. (HR-EV-048)

#### 5.4.7 Results of the challenge-testing programme

The results of the three challenge runs are summarised below. Counts are given as log colony forming units per gram of product. The acceptance criterion for the programme was that the *Listeria monocytogenes* count should not increase by more than 1.0 log over the full shelf life at 8 degrees Celsius, which is the abuse temperature used in the protocol.

**Run C1 (modified-atmosphere packaging only, no antimicrobial).** At 8 degrees Celsius the count rose from 2.0 log at day 0 to 3.6 log at day 12, an increase of 1.6 log. The run did not meet the acceptance criterion and was used to confirm that packaging alone was insufficient.

**Run C2 (modified-atmosphere packaging and cultured dextrose at 0.4% by weight).** At 8 degrees Celsius the count rose from 2.1 log at day 0 to 2.8 log at day 12, an increase of 0.7 log. The run met the acceptance criterion. Sensory assessment noted a faint tang from day 10 onwards, which the sensory panel judged to be above the preferred level for the braised product.

**Run C3 (modified-atmosphere packaging and cultured dextrose at 0.3% by weight, with revised water activity).** The results of the final run are set out in Table 5.4.7 below.

| Sampling point (8 degrees Celsius) | *L. monocytogenes* (log cfu/g) | Water activity | pH |
|---|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | 2.1 | 0.979 | 5.4 |
| Day 5 | 2.2 | 0.978 | 5.4 |
| Day 9 | 2.4 | 0.977 | 5.3 |

Run C3 showed an increase of 0.8 log at day 12, within the acceptance criterion. Sensory assessment of the run recorded no tang at day 0 and only a slight, acceptable note at day 12.

#### 5.4.8 Outcome of project P3

The outcome of project P3 is a validated shelf life of twelve days for the chilled braised product, achieved through modified-atmosphere packaging at 60% carbon dioxide and 40% nitrogen, cultured dextrose at 0.3% by weight, and a revised water activity target. The Company has issued a revised product specification covering packaging, recipe and shelf life, and the product has been produced to that specification since March 2025.

The uncertainty over whether the combination would control *Listeria monocytogenes* over twelve days was resolved by run C3. The uncertainty over flavour and texture was resolved through the sensory panel, which found the product acceptable at the revised cultured dextrose level. The uncertainty over the optimal antimicrobial concentration was resolved by comparing runs C2 and C3, which established 0.3% as the lowest level that met the acceptance criterion with an acceptable flavour.

The Company's view is that the outcome represents a material advance in its knowledge of how to apply modified-atmosphere packaging and natural antimicrobials to a braised, water-rich chilled product. Before the programme, the Company had no validated data on the behaviour of *Listeria* in its own products, and no means of predicting the effect of a given antimicrobial level on a given recipe. The validated data and the associated protocol now allow the Company to take further products through the same process with a defined method.

The Company acknowledges that the underlying techniques, namely modified-atmosphere packaging, refrigeration and the use of cultured dextrose, are established in the chilled food sector. The advance lies in the application to this product, the determination of the operating window, and the validation of the outcome against the Company's own acceptance criteria.
