### 3.6 Summary of the advance sought

Throughout the claim period the Company sought to achieve an appreciable improvement in the overall knowledge and capability in its field, resolving uncertainties that could not be readily deduced by a competent professional working in the field, in accordance with paragraphs 3, 6 and 13 of the DSIT Guidelines.

### 3.7 Project 1: Development of an innovative concealed-hinge bespoke larder system

**Main field of science or technology.** Materials and product design for bespoke furniture and joinery, including mechanical fixings and internal carcass layout.

**Baseline.** Traditional joinery practice for bespoke larder units, using established carcass construction, conventional butt hinges and standard shelving layouts as used across the bespoke trade.

**Advance sought.** The Company sought to achieve an advance in the design of a larder unit with a new internal layout and concealed hinge arrangement, delivering a distinctive product that offers greater storage access and a cleaner external appearance than traditional joinery practice. The Company's aim was to resolve the technical question of how a commercially available concealed hinge could be integrated into a bespoke carcass without loss of structural performance or finish quality.

**Uncertainties.** Whether the design would meet customer expectations for access and appearance. Whether the hinge arrangement could carry the loads expected of a full-height larder door over time. Achieving a premium finish within budget. Minimising material waste in the carcass and door components.

**How and when resolved.** Through successive prototype builds, each tested in the workshop and at two client homes for fit and function. Each iteration was measured against the success criteria set at the outset, and the results were recorded in the workshop log and the drawing folder. Client feedback from the two trial installations was used to adjust the hinge mounting at the bottom corner of the unit. The design was settled and documented in February 2025, and the specification is listed in the evidence index.

### 3.8 Project 2: Advanced CNC toolpath optimisation for hardwood components

**Main field of science or technology.** Manufacturing engineering, specifically computer-controlled machining of hardwood components.

**Baseline.** Traditional joinery practice for hardwood components, in which parts are shaped by hand and with conventional machine tools, supported by standard toolpath settings supplied with machine software.

**Advance sought.** The Company sought to achieve an advance in the use of a newly acquired CNC router, by developing toolpath strategies for hardwood components that give consistent results across oak and walnut. The Company's objective was a repeatable process that would allow bespoke parts to be produced to a consistent standard without repeated hand correction.

**Uncertainties.** Whether the toolpaths could achieve the required surface quality on hardwood. Whether tool wear could be controlled across a production run. Achieving the required tolerances without excessive machining time. Minimising the waste of hardwood during set-up and test cutting.

**How and when resolved.** Through a series of test cutting runs recorded in the workshop log, followed by measurement of finished parts against the Company's tolerance specification. Separate parameter sets were established for oak and walnut after the grain-direction and burning problems were observed, and the tool change interval was set on the basis of measured wear. The resulting procedure was written into the workshop procedure file, and the toolpath strategy was adopted for production in March 2025.

### 3.9 Project 3: Sustainable finishing techniques

**Main field of science or technology.** Materials science and chemistry, specifically surface finishes and coatings for timber.

**Baseline.** Traditional joinery finishing practice, using solvent-based lacquers and oil finishes applied by hand, as widely used in the bespoke joinery trade.

**Advance sought.** The Company sought to achieve an advance in finishing technique by evaluating a water-based lacquer that offers a lower environmental impact while maintaining the appearance and durability that clients expect. The Company's aim was to establish whether the water-based system could be applied to bespoke work to the standard required.

**Uncertainties.** Whether the water-based lacquer would achieve a finish that clients would regard as premium. Whether the finish would perform in domestic kitchens subject to heat and moisture. Achieving the finish within budget, given the cost of the new material. Minimising material waste during application and sanding.

**How and when resolved.** Through finishing trials on sample boards and on completed client pieces, assessed for appearance, hardness and water resistance. The trials used a standing water test and a ring test, with pencil and scratch assessment of hardness. A sanding sequence, a sealing coat and a conditioning step for oak were introduced during the trials to address surface raise and cloudiness. The Company adopted the water-based system for standard work in March 2025, after a customer dresser in walnut had been finished and used in a domestic kitchen for a fortnight.
