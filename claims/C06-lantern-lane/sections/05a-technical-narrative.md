## 5. Technical Narrative

### 5.1 Introduction

This section describes the research and development undertaken by Lantern Lane Joinery Ltd in the accounting period from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025. It is written project by project. For each project it sets out the baseline, the advance sought, the uncertainties, the methodology, a chronological work log, the outcomes and the start and end boundaries of the project. A competent professional statement is given for each project.

Throughout the claim period the Company sought to achieve an appreciable improvement in the overall knowledge and capability in its field, resolving uncertainties that could not be readily deduced by a competent professional working in the field, in accordance with paragraphs 3, 6 and 13 of the DSIT Guidelines.

The Company is a bespoke kitchen and furniture maker. Its field is joinery, and its competent professionals are craftspeople who have trained in bench joinery and who understand how timber moves, how joints are cut and how finishes perform. The Company's projects were chosen because each involved a question that a joiner working in the bespoke trade could not answer from existing practice alone.

### 5.2 Approach

The Company's approach to each project followed the same cycle. A question was identified from client work or from workshop experience. A design or process was proposed. A prototype or test sample was built. The result was measured against defined success criteria, and the design or process was refined. The cycle was repeated until the success criteria were met or until the Company concluded that the question had been answered.

The Company recorded its work in workshop logs, job cards, sketch books, photographs and a shared folder of drawings. Each prototype or test was given a reference, a date and a short description of what was tested and what was observed. These records form the basis of the work log for each project.

The directors led the projects. Craig Lantern acted as competent professional for all three projects and was responsible for design decisions, for the setting of success criteria and for the assessment of results. Josh Pearce and Kieran Doyle carried out a large part of the build and test work. Michelle Lantern contributed to the administration of the projects, including the recording of materials, the ordering of supplies and the upkeep of the records used in this section.

Prototypes were iteratively developed, tested against defined success criteria, and refined based on observed performance. This approach was used on all three projects and is described in more detail in the individual project sections.

### 5.3 Project 1: Development of an innovative concealed-hinge bespoke larder system

#### 5.3.1 Field and context

Project 1 concerns the design of a bespoke larder unit. A larder is a full-height cabinet, usually located in a kitchen or utility room, that stores food and kitchen equipment. In the Company's bespoke work, larders are normally built with exposed or semi-concealed hinges and a fixed internal shelf layout, which the Company's joiners have refined over many years.

The project's question was how a larder could be built with a new internal layout and with concealed hinges that would allow the door to close flush with the carcass, while still meeting the Company's standards for structure, function and finish.

#### 5.3.2 Baseline

The baseline for Project 1 is traditional joinery practice for bespoke larder units. This practice uses established carcass construction, with dadoed or dowelled shelves, conventional butt hinges or rebated hinges and standard shelf spacings. The Company has built larders to this practice for more than a decade and regards it as the baseline against which the new design is judged.

#### 5.3.3 Advance sought

The advance sought was a larder unit with a new internal layout and a concealed hinge arrangement. The Company's aim was to provide greater storage access, with pull-out and adjustable internal fittings, and a cleaner external appearance with no visible hinge. The advance is an improvement on the traditional joinery baseline in that it solves the technical problem of integrating a commercially available concealed hinge into a bespoke carcass without loss of structural performance or finish quality. A competent joiner working from traditional practice would not be able to resolve this question without building and testing prototypes, because the interaction between hinge geometry, carcass thickness, door weight and the required reveal depends on the particular materials and tolerances used.

#### 5.3.4 Uncertainties

The uncertainties for Project 1 were as follows.

- Whether the design would meet customer expectations for access and appearance.
- Whether the hinge arrangement could carry the loads expected of a full-height larder door over a long period of use.
- Achieving a premium finish within budget.
- Minimising material waste in the carcass and door components.

The first uncertainty was the principal one. The Company could not determine in advance whether a larder with the proposed layout would be accepted by clients as an improvement on the existing product, or whether the new arrangement would produce doors that sat unevenly or sagged. The second uncertainty concerned the structural behaviour of the hinge and carcass combination. The Company could not calculate the expected performance from first principles, because the manufacturer's data assumed a standard carcass that was not representative of the Company's bespoke construction.

#### 5.3.5 Methodology

The Company's methodology was to build a series of prototype larder units, each incorporating one or more design changes, and to test each unit for fit, function and finish. The prototypes were built in the workshop using the Company's standard equipment, together with the CNC router for the carcass panels from December 2024. Each prototype was assessed against the success criteria set in advance. The criteria were:

- door alignment within a reveal tolerance of 1.5 millimetres across the full height of the unit;
- a door that opens and closes through 110 degrees without binding after 5,000 cycles on the test rig;
- a finish that passes the Company's visual inspection standard at the first and second coat;
- no more than a defined allowance of offcut waste per unit.

The prototypes were tested in the workshop and then installed in two client homes for a trial period. The client homes were chosen because they represented typical kitchen layouts, and the clients agreed to provide feedback on the operation of the doors and the internal fittings.

#### 5.3.6 Work log

- **3 June 2024.** Project opened. Craig Lantern set out the design brief and the success criteria. Initial sketches prepared, with three alternative internal layouts.
- **18 June 2024.** Iteration 1. Prototype carcass built in mixed oak and MDF to test the proposed hinge position. Door alignment measured at 4.1 millimetres at the top of the unit. Result recorded as unsatisfactory against the 1.5 millimetre criterion.
- **2 July 2024.** Iteration 2. Carcass modified to include a stiffening rail and revised hinge spacing. Alignment improved to 2.6 millimetres. Result recorded as partially satisfactory.
- **24 July 2024.** Iteration 3. Hinge type changed to a concealed hinge with a wider mounting plate. Alignment measured at 1.4 millimetres. Criterion met on the test unit.
- **15 August 2024.** Iteration 4. Full-height prototype built in walnut veneer ply with oak frame. Door opened and closed on the rig for 1,200 cycles without binding.
- **9 September 2024.** Iteration 5. Internal layout revised to include pull-out fittings and adjustable shelves. Fit tested with a sample set of crockery and small appliances.
- **21 October 2024.** Iteration 6. Finish trials carried out on the door faces. First coat showed minor blistering at the edge joints. Edge treatment revised.
- **11 November 2024.** Iteration 7. Second finish trial. Blistering eliminated. Finish assessed as meeting the visual inspection standard at the second coat.
- **9 December 2024.** Iteration 8. Carcass panels cut on the CNC router for the first time within this project. Panel accuracy compared with hand-cut panels from iteration 4. Prototype rebuilt using machined panels.
- **20 January 2025.** Iteration 9. Prototype installed at the first client home for a trial period of three weeks. Client feedback recorded. The client reported that the door sat slightly proud at the bottom corner on one side.
- **10 February 2025.** Iteration 10. Hinge mounting adjusted at the bottom corner on the trial unit. Revised unit installed at the second client home. Feedback recorded as positive on access and appearance.
- **14 February 2025.** Project closed for the purposes of this claim. Design settled and documented in the Company's drawing folder.

#### 5.3.7 Outcomes

The outcome of Project 1 was a larder design with a concealed hinge arrangement and a new internal layout. The design meets the Company's success criteria for alignment, cycle performance and finish. The door alignment tolerance was achieved on the production prototype, the cycle test was passed and the finish met the visual inspection standard.

The design was not adopted in the form tested at iteration 1 or 2. The Company's experience of those iterations was that the hinge position and carcass stiffness were critical to the outcome, and this knowledge has been incorporated into the Company's documented larder specification. The Company now offers the concealed-hinge larder as a standard option for bespoke work.

Prototypes were iteratively developed, tested against defined success criteria, and refined based on observed performance. The result is a design that a competent joiner could not have produced from traditional practice without the testing described above.

#### 5.3.8 Boundaries

Project 1 started on 3 June 2024, when the design brief was set and the first sketches were prepared. It ended on 14 February 2025, when the design was settled and documented. Work on the larder unit for standard client orders after that date is not included in the claim.

#### 5.3.9 Competent professional statement

Craig Lantern is a competent professional for Project 1. He holds the City & Guilds Level 3 Diploma in Bench Joinery and has been a practising joiner for more than twenty years. His knowledge of carcass construction, hinge systems and finishing practice was used to set the success criteria, to interpret the results of each iteration and to decide when the design had been settled. The competent professional would have been unable to resolve the uncertainties described above from existing knowledge alone, because the information available in the trade did not cover the combination of concealed hinge, bespoke carcass and new internal layout used by the Company.
