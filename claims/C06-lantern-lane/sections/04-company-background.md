## 4. Company Background

### 4.1 History and activities

Lantern Lane Joinery Ltd was incorporated in 2011 by Craig Lantern and Michelle Lantern to provide bespoke kitchen, furniture and joinery services to domestic and small commercial clients in Dorsetshire and the surrounding counties. The Company's work is commissioned individually. Each kitchen, dresser, wardrobe or freestanding piece is designed with the client, drawn up, cut, assembled and finished in the Company's workshop, and then installed on site.

The Company's core product range comprises fitted kitchens in solid oak, walnut and painted hardwood, bespoke larders and pantry units, library shelving, dressers and occasional furniture. Most of the Company's income comes from kitchen commissions, which typically take between six and fourteen weeks from design approval to installation. Repeat and referral business forms a substantial part of the order book.

The Company's craft is traditional joinery. Its workshop practice relies on mortise and tenon, dovetail and frame-and-panel construction, supplemented by conventional machine tools including a table saw, planer thicknesser, router table and bandsaw. The Company has been recognised locally for the quality of its finish and for its willingness to adapt designs to the constraints of older houses.

### 4.2 Premises

The Company operates from Unit 2, Lantern Lane Industrial Estate, Wexcombe, Dorsetshire ZZ9 4LL. The unit provides approximately 1,400 square feet of workshop space, a timber store, a finishing booth with extraction, a small showroom and an office. The unit is leased on commercial terms and the lease runs until 2031.

The workshop layout was reorganised in the summer of 2024 to accommodate a computer-controlled router, which was installed in the Company's machining bay in August 2024. The finishing booth was relocated at the same time to give the finishing process its own ventilated space.

### 4.3 Ownership and governance

The issued share capital of the Company consists of ordinary shares held as follows: Craig Lantern holds 60 per cent and Michelle Lantern holds 40 per cent. Both are directors and both are actively involved in the day-to-day operation of the business. Craig Lantern leads on design, estimating and client relations and supervises the workshop. Michelle Lantern serves as Company Secretary and manages the Company's finances, payroll, supplier accounts and administrative records, including bookkeeping.

The Company has no group structure and holds no interests in other companies. It has no subsidiaries, joint ventures or associated undertakings.

### 4.4 Staff

The Company has four people: two directors, one joiner and one apprentice joiner. The joiner, Josh Pearce, joined the Company in 2016 and has been a member of the workshop team since then. He is responsible for the majority of the Company's cutting, assembly and installation work. The apprentice, Kieran Doyle, joined in September 2023 on a three-year apprenticeship in bench joinery, which he is undertaking with a local further education college. Kieran Doyle works alongside Josh Pearce in the workshop and attends the college on a day-release basis.

The directors' remuneration in the period consisted of a salary and dividends. Salary costs for all four members of staff are shown in the Costs section, together with employer National Insurance Contributions and employer pension contributions for the period.

### 4.5 Turnover and financial position

Turnover for the period ended 31 March 2025 was £640,000. The Company is profitable and has paid Corporation Tax in each of the last three accounting periods. Its net assets are modest, reflecting the nature of a workshop business with a small fixed asset base and a stock of timber held against customer orders.

The Company's trading position is stable. The order book at the date of approval of the accounts stood at a level consistent with the previous two years.

### 4.6 Funding

The Company has funded its activities from retained profits and from a commercial overdraft facility with its bank. The Company has not received any government grants, public subsidies or loans in connection with the three projects described in this pack. The Company has not previously received any research and development tax relief.

The purchase of the CNC router in August 2024 was financed from retained profits and from an equipment finance agreement with a commercial lender. The van used for deliveries and site visits is held under a lease. Both arrangements are described in the Costs section.

### 4.7 Approach to the R&D claim

In May 2025 the Company was approached by telephone by Quorlane R&D Partners Ltd. The caller asked: "Did you know your business could be eligible for thousands in government tax credits?" The Company's directors arranged a meeting with the agent later that month. The agent reviewed the Company's work over the period and identified the three projects described in this pack. The agent has prepared the claim documents, including the narrative, the cost schedules and the supporting appendices, on the basis of information provided by the Company.

The directors have reviewed the work described in this pack and are satisfied that it reflects the activities undertaken by the Company during the period.

### 4.8 Relationship with the agent

Quorlane R&D Partners Ltd is an independent firm of R&D tax relief consultants. The agent's engagement with the Company is governed by a letter of engagement, an extract of which is included in the Appendices. The agent is responsible for the preparation of the claim and for liaising with the Company on the information required.
