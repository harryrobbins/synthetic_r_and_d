## 6. Costs

### 6.1 Introduction

This section sets out the qualifying expenditure claimed by Lantern Lane Joinery Ltd for the accounting period from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025. It explains how each cost was identified, how the staff time was apportioned to the three projects, and how the relief has been computed. The cost tables are reproduced from the Company's cost schedule for the period. The period's National Insurance basis is 2024-25, and the scheme claimed is the merged scheme for a profitable company.

### 6.2 Scope of the claim

The claim covers two categories of expenditure: staff costs and other qualifying expenditure. Staff costs are the costs of the directors and employees who carried out the projects, including their salaries, employer National Insurance Contributions and employer pension contributions, multiplied by the proportion of their time spent on the projects. Other qualifying expenditure comprises consumables, software and other costs incurred directly in carrying out the projects.

### 6.3 Methodology for apportionment of staff costs

The Company recorded the time of its joiner and apprentice on weekly timesheets, which were signed off by the workshop supervisor each Friday. The timesheets record the hours worked on each job, including bespoke client commissions, workshop administration and project work. The proportion of each employee's time spent on the three projects was calculated from these records.

The directors' time was apportioned by a structured interview with the senior officer, Craig Lantern, held on 14 May 2025. The interview covered the directors' involvement in design, testing, supplier liaison and records for the period. The directors' time was also cross-checked against the workshop job cards and the Company's order book, which record the commissions on which each director worked. The proportions were agreed between the directors and the agent and are recorded in the Company's R&D apportionment file.

For Craig Lantern, who leads the design and development work, the apportionment is 100 per cent. For Michelle Lantern, whose role includes records and project administration in addition to her duties as Company Secretary, the apportionment is 80 per cent. For Josh Pearce, the joiner, the apportionment is 70 per cent. For Kieran Doyle, the apprentice, the apportionment is 70 per cent.

### 6.4 Remuneration of the directors

The staff costs for the directors include their remuneration for the period, which consisted of a salary and dividends. The directors' remuneration is shown in the staff table as a single figure, consistent with the Company's approach to director costs in the period. The dividend element is shown in the Note column of the staff table, so that the components of the figure are visible.

### 6.8 Explanation of other qualifying expenditure

**CNC router.** The Bramwell 2030 Pro router was purchased in August 2024 and installed in the machining bay for the purposes of Project 2. The router is used to produce test components and sample parts, and its cost is treated as consumable expenditure under the Company's costing policy for R&D work. The purchase invoice and the equipment finance agreement are listed in the Appendices.

**Oak, walnut, MDF and finishes.** The timber and finishing materials were used in the prototype builds for Project 1, the test cutting runs for Project 2 and the finishing trials for Project 3. Materials used in customer orders during the same period were recorded against the same supplier accounts, and the materials claimed represent the proportion attributable to the three projects.

**CAD/CAM licence.** The annual licence for the CAD/CAM package was used to design the toolpaths for Project 2 and the drawings for the Project 1 prototypes. The licence is a qualifying software cost because it was used directly in the projects.

**Van lease and fuel.** The van is used to transport prototypes to client homes for the trial installations described in Project 1 and to collect materials for the finishing trials in Project 3. The lease and fuel costs for the period are claimed in full on the basis that the van was used for these purposes.

### 6.9 Relief computation

The relief has been computed under the merged scheme for a profitable company, as an expenditure credit at 20 per cent of qualifying expenditure. The credit is taxable at the rate of Corporation Tax applicable to the Company, which is 25 per cent for the period. The net benefit to the Company is therefore approximately 15 per cent of qualifying expenditure.

| Item | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Total qualifying expenditure | £224,514 |
| Expenditure credit at 20% | £44,903 |
| Net benefit after Corporation Tax at 25% | £33,677 |

The expenditure credit is shown as a taxable credit in the Company's CT600 return for the period. The Company's turnover for the period was £640,000, which is the figure used for context in the Company's accounts and the agent's review.

### 6.10 Summary of the cost build-up

The staff costs of £132,814 are the sum of the four claimed staff figures in Section 6.5. The other qualifying expenditure of £91,700 is the sum of the five items in Section 6.6, comprising the router, the materials, the software licence and the van. The total qualifying expenditure of £224,514 is the sum of these two figures. The expenditure credit and the net benefit are calculated from that total as set out in Section 6.9. The figures have been reviewed by the directors and by the agent before inclusion in this pack, and no figure has been adjusted since the schedule was prepared.
