## 4. Company Background

### 4.1 Origins and purpose

Corvanta Bioscience Ltd was incorporated on 14 March 2021 to commercialise a line of research in low-temperature biocatalysis. The company was formed as a spin-out from the School of Chemistry at the University of Calderbridge, where its founding scientists had developed a method for engineering transaminase and ketoreductase enzymes to act on sterically demanding substrates. The founders wished to apply those enzymes to the manufacture of pharmaceutical intermediates, where biocatalysis can replace multi-step chemical routes that depend on heavy metals and high temperatures.

The company's business is built around one technical problem. Biocatalysts are attractive for continuous-flow manufacture, in which a substrate is pumped through a packed bed of immobilised enzyme. In practice, enzymes immobilised by the methods available to industry lose their activity quickly under flow conditions, and a bed that lasts only a few hundred hours is not economic for a commercial plant. The company's research is directed at making immobilised enzymes that last much longer, while keeping the activity and selectivity that make them useful in the first place.

### 4.2 Business model and revenue

Corvanta does not yet sell a commercial product. Its near-term route to revenue is licensing immobilised biocatalyst formats to pharmaceutical manufacturers and contract manufacturers, and selling engineered enzyme panels for use in process development. In the period to 31 March 2025 the company's only revenue came from paid feasibility studies for pharmaceutical customers, which totalled £210,000. These studies used the company's established screening protocols and are not part of the R&D described in this claim.

The company's commercial pipeline is described to investors in the company's annual strategy paper. It is not reproduced here, but the relevant point for this claim is that the company's commercial success depends on the same technical result that the R&D programme is trying to achieve. A longer-lived immobilised enzyme is the product, and the R&D programme is the route to it.

### 4.3 Ownership and group

The company's share capital is held by its founders, by a seed investor syndicate, and by employees under the company's share option scheme. The seed round of £4.2 million, completed in October 2022, was led by Ferncliff Ventures, a regional life-sciences fund, with participation from two business angels. No single shareholder holds a controlling interest. The company has no subsidiaries and is not a member of a group.

The company's board comprises Marcus Venn as chief executive and director, and two non-executive directors appointed under the seed investment agreement. Dr Imogen Saltash attends board meetings as chief scientific officer but is not a director.

### 4.4 Funding

The company has been funded by equity since incorporation. The seed round in October 2022 provided £4.2 million, which the company is applying over a period of approximately three years to its laboratory build-out, its staff, and its R&D programme.

The company also holds an Innovate UK grant of £1.1 million for a collaborative project called IMMOCAT, which runs from April 2023 to September 2025. The IMMOCAT grant supports workstreams on reactor integration and continuous manufacturing platforms, in collaboration with an engineering partner. The company discloses the grant in full in section 03. The company has considered the grant under the rules that apply to the ERIS scheme. It is the company's position that the grant does not reduce the qualifying expenditure claimed in this pack, because none of the grant-funded activity is included in the costs set out in section 06.

### 4.5 Staff

At the end of the period the company employed 14 people. Eight of them were engaged in R&D to a significant extent, and their roles and time allocations are set out in section 06. The remaining six comprise a finance manager, a business development lead, an office manager, and three part-time members of staff in administrative and sales support. None of the six is included in the claim, and none of their costs has been apportioned to R&D.

The R&D team is led by Dr Imogen Saltash, the chief scientific officer, who holds a doctorate in enzyme engineering and two postdoctoral appointments in biocatalysis. Dr Ravi Penhallow, senior scientist in enzyme engineering, leads the variant design and expression work. Dr Aoife Kernick, scientist in immobilisation chemistry, leads the support chemistry. Tomasz Brell and Hannah Quarles are research associates who carry out expression, purification and flow testing. Leon Fairhurst, analytical chemist, operates the company's HPLC and GC instruments. Priya Odedra, laboratory manager, maintains the R&D equipment and the laboratory's safety systems.

The chief executive, Marcus Venn, is an experienced commercial executive with a background in specialty chemicals. He attends the company's monthly technical steering meeting in addition to his commercial responsibilities. His claimed time is limited to that attendance.

### 4.6 Facilities

The company occupies Unit 7 at Fenhurst Science Park, Calderbridge ZZ24 9XQ. The laboratory extends to 420 square metres and includes a wet laboratory with fume extraction, a cold room, two packed-bed flow rigs rated to 6 bar, an analytical suite with HPLC and GC, and a small cell culture area for enzyme expression. The company's computing is based on a local workstation and on cloud services provided by a UK-based provider. The GPU compute used for predictive modelling is purchased on demand.

The laboratory's metered circuits are configured so that the R&D rigs and the analytical suite can be separately monitored. This arrangement is used to apportion utilities costs, as described in section 06.

### 4.7 Intellectual property

The company owns its own patent application, GB 2410517.4, filed on 14 November 2024 and relating to immobilised transaminase supports. The company has also obtained a licence from the University of Calderbridge for certain background enzyme sequences, on the university's standard commercial terms. The licence fee is not a qualifying cost and is not included in this claim.

### 4.8 Previous R&D claims

The company has made two previous R&D tax relief claims. The first covered the accounting period ended 31 March 2023 under the SME scheme and was submitted on 27 January 2024. The second covered the accounting period ended 31 March 2024 and was submitted on 18 December 2024. Both claims were accepted. The period covered by this claim follows directly from the second.
