### 3.8 Project 1: CVB-P1 "LONGFLOW"

**Main field of science or technology.** Biotechnology, specifically biochemical engineering and enzyme immobilisation for continuous-flow processing.

**Baseline (current knowledge and practice).** Commercially available immobilised transaminases, typically attached to epoxy-methacrylate resins, lose 40 to 70% of their activity within 100 to 150 hours in packed-bed flow at 30 to 40 °C. The losses arise from leaching of the pyridoxal-5'-phosphate cofactor and from dissociation of the dimeric enzyme into inactive subunits. Published approaches improve stability but reduce initial activity by 50 to 60% and do not translate to rigid supports rated for pressures above 5 bar. The baseline is described in full in section 05.

**Advance sought.** A support chemistry combined with surface-lysine engineering of the enzyme, which keeps the dimer interface cross-linked and retains the cofactor in situ. The target is retained activity above 80% at 500 hours, with initial activity at least 70% of that of the free enzyme.

**Technical uncertainties.** (1) Whether the surface lysine substitutions needed for oriented attachment would destabilise the dimer interface or alter the geometry of the active site. (2) Whether the cofactor could be retained without blocking the active site. (3) Whether a pore architecture compatible with high-pressure flow would allow sufficient enzyme loading without diffusion limitation.

**How the uncertainties were resolved, and when.** Uncertainty (1) was addressed through a variant panel from April to July 2024, using size-exclusion chromatography with multi-angle light scattering to confirm dimer integrity, and circular dichroism and activity assays to check active-site geometry. Uncertainty (2) was tested through a polyethyleneimine co-immobilisation route, which the company abandoned in August 2024 because of a loss of selectivity. Uncertainty (3) was addressed by moving to a hierarchical silica support in October 2024. By March 2025 the company had demonstrated 612 hours of continuous flow at 83% retained activity, and initial activity of 74% of free enzyme. Two of the three uncertainties are partly resolved. A scale-up uncertainty remains open into the financial year ending 31 March 2026.

**Dates of work.** From 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025, continuing feasibility work carried out in the previous period.

### 3.9 Project 2: CVB-P2 "PREDICTA"

**Main field of science or technology.** Computational biology, specifically predictive modelling of enzyme activity after immobilisation.

**Baseline.** Structure-prediction and docking tools predict the structure of a free enzyme, but no validated method exists for predicting the activity an enzyme retains after covalent multipoint attachment. Practitioners screen empirically, which is slow and costly when a panel contains hundreds of variants.

**Advance sought.** A surrogate model trained on the company's own dataset of 340 immobilisation experiments, combined with coarse-grained molecular dynamics describing surface accessibility. The target is a Spearman rank correlation coefficient above 0.6 on held-out variants.

**Technical uncertainties.** (1) Whether a coarse-grained surface-accessibility signal carries sufficient information to predict retained activity. (2) Whether the dataset is large enough to train a model that generalises to new variants.

**How the uncertainties were resolved, and when.** The model was trained and evaluated between June 2024 and February 2025. On a held-out set of variants the correlation was 0.52. The model is useful for pruning large variant panels before wet-laboratory work, but it did not meet the 0.6 target. The company has concluded that the coarse-grained signal carries useful information, but that the dataset is not yet large enough to resolve the second uncertainty. The project continues into the following period.

**Dates of work.** From 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025.
