## 6. Costs

### 6.1 Basis of preparation

This section sets out the qualifying expenditure claimed for the accounting period from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025. The figures are taken from the company's payroll records, its purchase ledger, its supplier invoices and its laboratory timesheets. They have been reconciled to the company's management accounts for the period. The National Insurance basis is the 2024-25 rates. The relief is claimed under the ERIS scheme, and the computation is set out in section 6.5.

Costs are presented in two schedules: staff costs (section 6.2) and other qualifying expenditure (section 6.3). The totals are given in section 6.4. Apportionment methods are described in sections 6.6 to 6.9.

### 6.5 Relief computation

The relief is computed under the ERIS scheme. The company is a loss-making, R&D-intensive small or medium-sized enterprise, and its R&D intensity for the period is 35.3%, which meets the intensity threshold for the scheme. The additional deduction is 86% of qualifying expenditure. The payable credit is 14.5% of the surrenderable loss.

The figures are as follows.

- Total qualifying expenditure: £599,709
- Additional deduction at 86%: extra deduction £515,750
- Surrenderable loss: £1,115,459
- Payable credit at 14.5% of surrenderable loss: £161,742
- Company turnover for context: £210,000
- Total relevant expenditure (claimed, for intensity): £1,700,000
- Intensity as claimed: 35.3%
- Trading loss before R&D deduction: £1,490,000

The company's turnover consists entirely of revenue from customer feasibility studies, as described in section 03.

### 6.6 Staff apportionment

Each employee in section 6.2 records their time weekly in the company's electronic laboratory notebook system, LabTrack, against project codes. The project codes are CVB-P1 and CVB-P2 for the two R&D projects, FEAS for customer feasibility studies, and ADMIN for administration, GMP documentation and other non-R&D activity. Only time recorded against CVB-P1 and CVB-P2 is claimed. Time recorded against FEAS and ADMIN is excluded in full.

Each employee's R&D percentage is derived from the timesheets for the period. The percentages were reconciled to the timesheets for each employee, and each reconciled figure falls within 2 percentage points of the timesheet total. The timesheets are summarised in the extract in section 07. Where an employee's recorded time was partly devoted to feasibility studies, that portion is excluded, and the R&D percentage reflects the balance.

Dr Saltash's claimed percentage of 85% reflects her direction of both projects, her technical review of each iteration, and her authorship of the competent professional statement in section 5.16. The 15% excluded relates to the feasibility studies that she oversaw in her commercial advisory capacity for customers.

Dr Penhallow, Dr Kernick, Tomasz Brell and Hannah Quarles work almost entirely on the laboratory and computational programme. Their percentages reflect the small amount of time they spent on customer feasibility work and on GMP documentation, which is excluded.

Leon Fairhurst operates the company's HPLC and chiral HPLC instruments. He runs both R&D and feasibility samples through the same instruments. His time on feasibility samples, recorded against FEAS, has been excluded, and the claimed percentage of 70% reflects the time recorded against CVB-P1 and CVB-P2.

### 6.7 Chief executive and laboratory manager

The company's chief executive, Marcus Venn, is claimed at 10% of his total cost. The claim relates to his attendance at the monthly technical steering meeting, at which the company's R&D plan is reviewed and the next phase of each project is planned. The meetings are recorded in the steering committee minutes for each month of the period, referenced CVB-SC-2024-04 to CVB-SC-2025-03. The minutes record his contribution to decisions on the switch of support architecture, the closure of the polyethyleneimine route, and the scope of the variant panels. The remaining 90% of his time is commercial and administrative, and it is excluded. The claim for the chief executive's time is limited to the technical planning activity described, and it is consistent with the qualifying activity of planning a qualifying indirect activity under the Guidelines.

The laboratory manager, Priya Odedra, is claimed at 40% of her total cost. Her claimed activity is the maintenance of the company's R&D equipment, including the two packed-bed flow rigs, the cold room and the HPLC maintenance schedules, and the monitoring of the laboratory's safety systems, including the high-pressure interlocks. This work directly supports the R&D described in section 05, since the flow rigs and the cold room are used only for the company's R&D and the CVB-P1 long-run tests could not be carried out without them. The claimed 40% is recorded against CVB-P1 and CVB-P2 in her timesheets, and the remaining 60% is recorded against ADMIN for general laboratory administration and the receipt of goods. The claim for her time is a qualifying indirect activity.

### 6.8 Consumables

The consumables of £148,400 comprise enzymes, resins, substrates, solvents and chromatography columns. They are drawn from the company's purchase ledger, from the nominal codes 5100 to 5140, and the purchases are recorded against the R&D project codes at the time of issue. Stock drawn from the same codes for customer feasibility jobs has been excluded from the figure claimed. That excluded stock amounts to £23,600.

Stock that was drawn for R&D and then used for feasibility work, or the reverse, was reallocated on the basis of the lab book entry for the job. The company's inventory records are maintained against the lab book entries for each draw, so that a draw can be traced to the project for which it was made.

### 6.9 Utilities, subcontract and externally provided workers

Lab utilities of £11,900 comprise electricity, water and cooling costs for the laboratory. Each R&D circuit is separately metered, and the R&D rigs and the analytical suite are drawn from circuits that are monitored by sub-meters. The company has apportioned the metered consumption of those circuits to R&D, and the apportioned figure is claimed. The remaining consumption of the laboratory, which serves office and feasibility functions, is excluded.

The subcontract with the University of Calderbridge covers cryo-EM structural work carried out on the company's behalf during the period, for a total contract value of £56,000. The work was carried out on the basis of a statement of work, which identifies the structural questions to be addressed in each phase. The statement of work distinguishes between structural questions that bear directly on the uncertainties described in section 05 and questions relating to routine characterisation, which the company does not claim. The company has claimed 65% of the contract value on the basis of that distinction. The subcontract was placed on the university's standard commercial terms, and the invoices are supported by the university's time records. The subcontractor is unconnected with the company for the purposes of the claim.

The agency laboratory technician, costing £28,500 for the period, worked on the company's flow rigs and on the immobilisation protocol. The technician was supplied by an employment agency on a standard commercial contract, and the company has claimed 65% of the cost on the basis of the technician's timesheets. The technician's time on feasibility sample preparation has been excluded.

### 6.10 Software and data

The molecular modelling licence, costing £14,200, is claimed at 100% R&D use. It is used for the coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulations and the surface-accessibility calculations that form the basis of CVB-P2, and for the structure analyses that inform the variant design for CVB-P1. The licence was not used for customer feasibility work.

The GPU compute cost of £9,300 was incurred on demand for the coarse-grained simulations described in section 5.14. The compute logs are referenced as CVB-CM-2024-06 and the subsequent entries, and the invoices are listed in section 07. These costs are attributed to CVB-P2.

### 6.11 Costs excluded from the claim

The company has excluded from the claim costs of £61,000 in total. These comprise the stock of £23,600 drawn for feasibility jobs, as described in section 6.8, together with staff time and other costs recorded against FEAS and ADMIN codes for customer feasibility, GMP documentation and general administration. The exclusion is applied in the timesheets and purchase records, so that the amounts in sections 6.2 and 6.3 are net of these costs.

No patent costs are included in the claim, and the costs of the patent application GB 2410517.4 are excluded in full. Likewise, no costs of the company's conference attendance, marketing or licence fees for background enzyme sequences are included.

### 6.12 Cost schedule reconciliation

The staff costs in section 6.2 reconcile to the payroll records for the period. The other qualifying expenditure in section 6.3 reconciles to the purchase ledger, the supplier invoices, and the utilities meter records. The subtotal of £360,984 and the subtotal of £238,725 sum to the total qualifying expenditure of £599,709 in section 6.4. The extra deduction, surrenderable loss and payable credit in section 6.5 are computed from that total in accordance with the ERIS scheme.

The company's accounting records, timesheets and invoices are available to HMRC on request, and the evidence index in section 07 identifies each source.
