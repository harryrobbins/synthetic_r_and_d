### 5.11 Project 2: CVB-P2 "PREDICTA" – overview

CVB-P2 addresses a different problem from CVB-P1. Where CVB-P1 seeks a more stable immobilised enzyme, CVB-P2 seeks a way of predicting, before any wet-laboratory work is done, how much activity a given enzyme variant will retain after covalent multipoint attachment. The company's variant panels can contain hundreds of candidates. Each candidate must currently be expressed, purified, immobilised and tested in flow, which takes several weeks per panel. A validated predictor would allow the panel to be pruned before the laboratory work begins, and it would concentrate the company's laboratory capacity on the variants most likely to succeed.

The project is distinct from the computational work that the company routinely carries out in support of its enzyme engineering, which uses structure prediction and docking to propose candidate mutations. Those tools predict the structure of the free enzyme. They do not predict the activity retained after immobilisation, and the company's experience is that the two are only loosely correlated. The company's dataset of immobilisation experiments is therefore the essential resource for CVB-P2, and no equivalent public dataset of comparable size and consistency was available at the start of the period.

### 5.12 Baseline for CVB-P2

At the start of the period, structure-prediction and docking tools could predict the three-dimensional structure of a free enzyme with good accuracy, and they could estimate the binding energy of a substrate or cofactor in the active site. Those capabilities are well established. There was no validated method, in the published literature or in commercial software, for predicting the fraction of activity an enzyme retains after covalent immobilisation on a porous support. The effects of immobilisation depend on the position of the attachment points relative to the dimer interface, the flexibility of the surface, the local chemistry of the support, and the coupled effects of those factors under flow. None of these is captured by a free-enzyme structure.

In practice, the field screens empirically. A candidate panel is immobilised and tested, and the best performers are taken forward. The company's own feasibility work for CVB-P1 followed this approach, and it confirmed that a panel of twelve variants, which takes approximately six weeks to express, immobilise and test, can yield a single useful lead. The baseline for CVB-P2 is therefore an empirical screening process that is correct in its outcome but slow and expensive, and that offers no predictive guidance before the experiments are run.

### 5.13 Advance, uncertainties and methodology for CVB-P2

The advance sought is a surrogate model trained on the company's dataset of 340 immobilisation experiments, with inputs that describe the free-enzyme structure, the proposed attachment points, and the surface accessibility of those points computed by coarse-grained molecular dynamics. The company's hypothesis is that accessibility, which is the extent to which an attachment point and its neighbourhood are exposed to the support and to the solvent during flow, carries information about retained activity that the free-enzyme structure does not. The target was a Spearman rank correlation coefficient above 0.6 between predicted and measured retained activity on held-out variants. Rank correlation was chosen because the company's use of the model is to rank candidates for pruning, and an absolute error measure would have been less relevant to that use.

Two uncertainties were identified at the start of the period. The first was whether a coarse-grained surface-accessibility signal would carry enough information to predict retained activity, given that coarse-grained models necessarily omit fine atomic detail. The second was whether the dataset of 340 experiments was large enough to train a model that generalised to new variants, or whether the model would learn the particular features of the company's own variant families and fail on new families. Neither uncertainty could be resolved by a competent professional from the published literature, since the required combination of data and modelling had not been reported.

The dataset was assembled from the company's laboratory records for the years to 31 March 2024 and for the period. Each record gives the variant identity, the attachment chemistry, the bed configuration, the initial activity and the retained activity at fixed time points. The records were standardised in April 2024 by Dr Kernick and Dr Penhallow, and each was checked against the original laboratory page. Records with missing or inconsistent fields were excluded, and the final dataset of 340 experiments was locked for training on 30 April 2024.

The modelling methodology has three components. The first is a feature set. Structure-derived features were computed from the free-enzyme structure, including solvent-accessible surface area, local flexibility from the crystallographic B-factors, and distance to the dimer interface. The second is a coarse-grained molecular dynamics simulation of each variant, run on the company's GPU compute, from which a surface-accessibility feature was extracted for each attachment point. The third is a surrogate model, a Gaussian process regressor with an automatic relevance determination kernel, which was chosen because it provides uncertainty estimates alongside its predictions and because the dataset was small. A gradient-boosted tree model was used as a comparator.

Evaluation was carried out by five-fold cross-validation during development, and then by a held-out set of variants that were withheld from training until the model was frozen. The held-out set was designed to contain variant families not represented in the training set, so that the second uncertainty could be tested directly.

### 5.14 Project 2 work log

**1 April 2024 (CVB-LB-0418).** Dataset assembly began. The company's immobilisation records for the years to 31 March 2024 were gathered and transcribed into a common format. The initial assembly contained 287 experiments. Dr Kernick recorded in the page that a further 53 experiments from the period would be added as they were completed.

**22 April 2024 (CVB-LB-0447).** Feature set design. Dr Penhallow proposed structure-derived features for each variant and attachment point, and the team agreed the list. The list was reviewed against the physical reasoning for each feature, so that no feature was included solely because it improved a fit. Solvent-accessible surface area and distance to the dimer interface were retained. Crystallographic B-factors were retained as a proxy for flexibility. Eleven further features were considered and rejected for lack of physical motivation.

**20 May 2024 (CVB-LB-0493).** First model, using structure-derived features only. Five-fold cross-validation gave a Spearman correlation of 0.21. The result was consistent with the expectation that the free-enzyme structure alone would be an incomplete predictor. The team recorded that the structure-only model was a baseline for the subsequent work and that a correlation of 0.21 was not useful for pruning.

**17 June 2024 (CVB-LB-0566; CVB-CM-2024-06).** Coarse-grained molecular dynamics set up for sixty variants, selected to span the range of attachment chemistries and dimer positions in the dataset. Simulations were run on the company's GPU compute in batches, with each run logged in the compute record. The first batch of twelve variants was completed by the end of the month, and the accessibility feature was extracted for each attachment point.

**15 July 2024 (CVB-LB-0609).** Surface-accessibility feature added to the model for the sixty variants with simulation data. Five-fold cross-validation gave a Spearman correlation of 0.41 on that subset. The improvement over the structure-only model was taken as evidence that accessibility carries information about retained activity. The team noted that the improvement was substantial but that the subset was small, and that the model had not yet been tested on the full dataset.

**12 September 2024 (CVB-LB-0703).** Extension of the coarse-grained runs to all variants with attachment-point data. The dataset was examined for coverage in lysine-substitution space. The team found that coverage was sparse for variants with three or more substitutions, and that most variants in the dataset came from families sharing a common substitution pattern. This finding was relevant to the second uncertainty, and the team recorded that the held-out evaluation would need to include variants from different families. Cross-validation on the full dataset gave a Spearman correlation of 0.44 with the Gaussian process model.

**14 October 2024 (CVB-LB-0741).** Gaussian process model adjusted for the change in support architecture from mesoporous silica and epoxy-methacrylate to hierarchical silica. The architecture was encoded as a categorical feature. Cross-validation performance was unchanged at 0.44, and the team concluded that the architecture feature was not the limiting factor. The result was discussed with the CVB-P1 team, who agreed to record support architecture for all future experiments so that the dataset could be extended consistently.

**10 December 2024 (CVB-LB-0822).** First held-out evaluation. Forty variants from families not represented in the training set were withheld from training, and the model was frozen on the remaining 300 experiments. The Spearman correlation on the held-out variants was 0.47. The team recorded that this was below the target of 0.6 and that the second uncertainty might be limiting. The model's predictive intervals were checked and found to be wider for the held-out families, which the team regarded as a sign that the model was correctly reporting its lower confidence.

**6 January 2025 (CVB-LB-0834).** Recalibration of the coarse-grained simulations, using a revised set of solvent parameters that the team had reviewed against the literature. Simulations for the held-out variants were repeated under the revised parameters. The Spearman correlation on the held-out set rose to 0.49. The team concluded that the improvement was small but genuine, and that the coarse-grained signal was sensitive to parameter choice in a way that required further work.

**14 February 2025 (CVB-LB-0872).** Final held-out evaluation for the period. A second held-out set of 52 variants was assembled, including the V2 panel variants immobilised in December 2024 for CVB-P1, which were the most recent data and were not in the training set. The model was frozen before evaluation. The Spearman correlation on this set was 0.52. The result was below the target of 0.6. The team noted that the model's ranking was useful for pruning, since the top quartile of predictions contained the majority of the variants with retained activity above 80% at 96 hours, but that the model did not meet the target for rank correlation across the full set.

**28 March 2025 (CVB-LB-0908).** Project review. Dr Saltash reviewed the two uncertainties against the outcome of the period. The coarse-grained accessibility signal was judged to carry useful information, because it improved the model in every evaluation in which it was tested. The dataset size was judged not yet sufficient to resolve the second uncertainty. The team agreed that the project would continue into the following period, with two actions: the dataset would be extended with the flow data from CVB-P1, and the held-out evaluation would be repeated on a larger set of families. The project status was recorded as partly successful.

### 5.15 Project 2 outcomes and boundaries

The outcome of CVB-P2 in the period is an honest partial result. The company's primary target, a Spearman correlation above 0.6 on held-out variants, was not met. The best held-out result was 0.52. The model is useful for pruning large variant panels, because its top predictions consistently contained variants that performed well in flow, but it is not yet a reliable ranking tool across the full range of variants. The company regards the result as an advance on the empirical baseline, since the structure-only model achieved 0.21 and the accessibility signal was shown to add information in every test. The company also regards the remaining gap as a genuine technical uncertainty, and the project continues.

The claim for CVB-P2 covers work from 1 April 2024 to 31 March 2025. The GPU compute for the coarse-grained simulations, costing £9,300 in the period, and the molecular modelling licence, costing £14,200 for the period at 100% R&D use, are attributed to CVB-P2. Work on the dataset that was carried out for CVB-P1 has been apportioned to CVB-P1 where it concerned the P1 flow results, and to CVB-P2 where it concerned the immobilisation records used for training. Routine structure-prediction and docking work carried out to propose candidate mutations for CVB-P1 is attributed to CVB-P1, and no costs of that work are claimed under CVB-P2.

Customer feasibility studies, which used the company's routine screening protocols, are excluded from CVB-P2 in the same way as from CVB-P1. No cost of the feasibility studies is included in the claim.
