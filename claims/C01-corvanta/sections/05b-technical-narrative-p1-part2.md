### 5.8 Project 1 work log: September 2024 to March 2025

The work log continues from the August 2024 review. In September the team moved from the abandoned coating route to a structured support-architecture study, and from there to a second variant panel designed around the support that had been selected.

**9 September 2024 (CVB-LB-0690).** Support screen of the mesoporous silica series. Dr Kernick prepared three silica supports with nominal pore diameters of 12, 20 and 30 nm, all of which were functionalised with the same amine-reactive linker used in the epoxy-methacrylate baseline. Enzyme loading was measured by protein assay of the supernatant before and after coupling. The 12 nm support carried 19 mg of enzyme per gram, which was too low for the target bed density. The 20 nm and 30 nm supports carried 28 and 31 mg per gram. Under the 5 bar test, the 30 nm support showed compression of the pore network, with a measured pressure drop increase of 37% over 24 hours, and the 20 nm support showed a smaller increase of 9%. The team concluded that mesoporous silica could reach adequate stability at 20 nm, but that enzyme loading would remain below the level needed for a commercially sized bed.

**23 September 2024 (CVB-LB-0712).** First test of the hierarchical silica prototype. The prototype combined mesopores of approximately 15 nm for enzyme loading with an interconnected macropore network of approximately 800 nm for mass transfer and for pressure tolerance. Enzyme loading was 45 mg per gram, the highest yet measured on a rigid support. The variant V1-05 was immobilised on the prototype and run at 35 °C and 5 bar. Initial activity was 64% of free enzyme and retained activity at 72 hours was 79%. The macropore network prevented the pressure build-up observed on the mesoporous supports. The team recorded a clear signal that the architecture had potential, but a 72-hour test is not long enough to judge stability against the 500-hour target.

**14 October 2024 (CVB-LB-0740; CVB-SC-2024-10).** Decision to adopt hierarchical silica as the primary support for CVB-P1. The steering committee reviewed the September results alongside the August selectivity data. The composite silica-polymer shell route was deprioritised because its enzyme loading was lower than that of the hierarchical prototype and because it required an additional coating step that raised the risk of selectivity loss. The polyethyleneimine route was formally closed. The committee approved a second variant panel designed specifically for the hierarchical silica support, and it approved a dedicated flow rig for the work.

**28 October 2024 (CVB-LB-0758).** Diffusion study on the hierarchical silica support. A bed of V1-05 was loaded at four levels, from 15 to 40 mg of enzyme per millilitre of bed volume, and each bed was run at 6 bar. The results showed that initial activity increased with loading up to 25 mg per millilitre, and then levelled off. Above that loading the apparent activity of the bed fell, and the team attributed the fall to diffusion limitation within the mesopores at the bed scale. The design target for enzyme loading was therefore set at 25 mg per millilitre of bed, which is below the maximum possible loading but above the level needed for a commercially sized bed.

**11 November 2024 (CVB-LB-0779).** Uncertainty 2 work on the hierarchical silica support. Two strategies for PLP retention were compared. In the first, PLP was fed continuously with the substrate. In the second, PLP was loaded onto the support before enzyme immobilisation, so that it was held within the pore network close to the immobilised enzyme. Continuous feeding gave retained activity of 66% at 96 hours and consumed PLP at a rate that was not acceptable for a commercial process. Pre-loading gave a PLP leaching rate of 2% over 96 hours in a static test, and retained activity of 74% at 96 hours in flow. Chiral HPLC confirmed enantiomeric excess of 99.3%, which matched the free enzyme. The pre-loading strategy was adopted for the second variant panel.

**2 December 2024 (CVB-LB-0801).** Surface lysine design round 2. The second variant panel, V2, was designed using the results of the first panel and the hierarchical silica support. Dr Penhallow kept the lysine positions that had been tolerated in V1-02, V1-05 and V1-09, and excluded the positions associated with aggregation in V1-07 and V1-11. The panel contained eight variants, V2-01 to V2-08, each with two to four surface lysine substitutions. The panel was designed to test the combined effect of the best-tolerated positions and the new support architecture.

**9 December 2024 (CVB-LB-0814).** Immobilisation of the V2 panel on the hierarchical silica support using the pre-loaded PLP protocol. Seven variants immobilised without visible aggregation. V2-06 aggregated during the coupling step and was dropped from the panel. Initial activity of the seven was measured against the free enzyme. V2-04, which carries four surface lysine substitutions at positions on the monomer face opposite the active site, gave 61% of free-enzyme activity. V2-01 gave 55%, and the remaining five variants gave between 38% and 52%.

**16 December 2024 (CVB-LB-0826).** Structural checks on V2-04. SEC-MALS confirmed that the immobilised dimer was intact, with measured molar mass within 3% of the expected dimer mass. Circular dichroism of the free enzyme showed no significant change from the parent enzyme. A 96-hour screen in flow gave retained activity of 88% at 96 hours on the hierarchical silica support, the best result yet at that time point. The team agreed that V2-04 would be the lead variant for the long-run test, subject to improving its initial activity.

**13 January 2025 (CVB-LB-0838).** Optimisation of the immobilisation protocol for V2-04. Dr Kernick varied the coupling pH, the PLP pre-loading time and the washing sequence in a designed experiment of twelve runs. The main effect on initial activity was the PLP pre-loading time. Shorter pre-loading gave higher initial activity but lower PLP retention, and the optimum lay between 40 and 50 minutes. Two conditions gave initial activity above 70% and PLP leaching below 3% over 96 hours, and one of them was selected for the long-run test.

**20 January 2025 (CVB-LB-0843).** Initial activity of V2-04 on the optimised protocol measured at 74% of free-enzyme activity, against the target of at least 70%. Enantiomeric excess was 99.4%. Three independent immobilisation batches were prepared with the same protocol, and their initial activities were 73%, 74% and 75%, which the team regarded as sufficiently reproducible for the long-run test.

**3 February 2025 (CVB-LB-0852).** Start of the long-run flow test on V2-04. A bed of 2.0 mL was packed with the optimised immobilised enzyme and run at 35 °C and 5 bar with a substrate solution at the standard concentration. The test was started at 09:00 and logged continuously for pressure, temperature and flow. Samples were taken at 24-hour intervals and at each interval the outlet was assayed by HPLC.

**10 February 2025 (CVB-LB-0866).** Long-run test at 168 hours. Retained activity was 93% of initial activity. Enantiomeric excess remained above 99%. Pressure drop across the bed was stable at 0.9 bar, and no change in bed volume was observed.

**17 February 2025 (CVB-LB-0877).** Long-run test at 336 hours. Retained activity was 89%. The team noted that the rate of activity loss had increased slightly between 168 and 336 hours, and Dr Saltash asked for sampling to be increased to every 12 hours for the remainder of the run. The additional sampling showed no abrupt change in activity, and the increase in the rate of loss was consistent with a gradual process.

**23 February 2025 (CVB-LB-0884; CVB-SC-2025-02).** Long-run test at 500 hours. Retained activity was 85%, against the target of more than 80% at 500 hours. Enantiomeric excess was 99.2%. The steering committee reviewed the result at its February meeting and recorded that the 500-hour target had been met. The committee noted that the result had been achieved on a laboratory-scale bed, and that the scale-up uncertainty had not yet been tested.

**28 February 2025 (CVB-LB-0891).** Long-run test at 612 hours, the latest point reached in the test. Retained activity was 83%, with initial activity of 74% of free enzyme and enantiomeric excess of 99.1%. The test was stopped on 1 March 2025 so that the rig could be reassigned to the scale-up comparison described below. The column and its data were retained for analysis.

**14 March 2025 (CVB-LB-0905).** Scale-up comparison. A batch of hierarchical silica support was prepared from a precursor synthesised at approximately one kilogram scale, roughly 100 times the scale of the laboratory preparations. The enzyme loading on the scale-up batch was 34 mg per gram, compared with 45 mg per gram on the laboratory batches. The team considered that the difference was probably due to changes in the precursor particle size distribution, and it recorded that the scale-up batch had not been run in flow. The question of whether the scale-up support could be brought to the laboratory loading, and whether the stability result would transfer, was recorded as open.

**27 March 2025 (CVB-LB-0909).** Project review for the financial year. The team reviewed the status of each uncertainty against the targets set at the start of the period. The 500-hour target and the initial activity target had been met on the laboratory-scale bed. Uncertainty 1 (dimer interface and active-site geometry) was regarded as resolved for the variants tested, since the dimer was retained and the active site was unaffected in each case. Uncertainty 2 (PLP retention) was regarded as partly resolved, because the pre-loading strategy worked on the laboratory bed but the PLP inventory on the scale-up batch had not yet been measured. Uncertainty 3 (pore architecture and diffusion) was regarded as partly resolved, because the architecture had been shown to work at laboratory scale but not at the scale required for a commercial bed.

**31 March 2025 (CVB-LB-0911).** Period-end summary entry for CVB-P1. The page records the final state of the project at the end of the claim period. The scale-up uncertainty remains open, and the work to resolve it is scheduled for the financial year ending 31 March 2026. Costs of that work are not included in this claim.

### 5.9 Project 1 outcomes

The outcomes of CVB-P1 in the period are as follows.

First, the company achieved its primary technical target on a laboratory-scale packed bed. The engineered transaminase immobilised on hierarchical silica retained 85% of its activity at 500 hours and 83% at 612 hours, at 35 °C and 5 bar. The baseline reported in the literature had lost 40 to 70% of activity by 150 hours. The measured result therefore exceeds the baseline on stability by a wide margin.

Second, the company achieved its target for initial activity. The optimised V2-04 preparation gave an initial activity of 74% of free enzyme, which is above the 70% target and above the 50 to 60% reduction that the published approaches imposed on initial activity. Enantiomeric excess was maintained at or above 99% throughout the long-run test.

Third, the company identified and tested a number of approaches that did not work. The polyethyleneimine co-immobilisation route reduced enantiomeric excess to 94% and was abandoned. The mesoporous silica supports either gave insufficient enzyme loading or compressed under pressure. Two of the twelve V1 variants aggregated during immobilisation, and the surface lysine positions associated with them were excluded from V2. Continuous PLP feeding was found to consume too much cofactor for a commercial process. Each of these results shaped the subsequent work.

Fourth, the company has not resolved the scale-up uncertainty. The scale-up batch of hierarchical silica gave a lower enzyme loading than the laboratory batches, and the stability of the scale-up material has not been tested in flow. The company regards this as a genuine technical uncertainty, because the support chemistry that works at laboratory scale has not yet been demonstrated at the scale of a commercial bed. The company's view is that the scale-up uncertainty will require further investigation, and it is planned for the financial year ending 31 March 2026.

The company's patent application GB 2410517.4, filed on 14 November 2024, describes the hierarchical silica support and the pre-loading protocol. The poster presented at BioTrans Northern Europe 2024 in September 2024 described the preliminary architecture results. Neither the patent nor the poster is a cost claimed in this pack.

### 5.10 Project 1 boundaries and exclusions

The claim for CVB-P1 starts on 1 April 2024. It continues feasibility work carried out in the previous period, but no costs from that period are claimed. The first claimed entry is the kick-off recorded on 3 April 2024 (CVB-LB-0412), and the last claimed entry is the period-end summary of 31 March 2025 (CVB-LB-0911). Work carried out after 31 March 2025, including the scale-up work, is outside the claim.

The following activities are excluded from CVB-P1, and no costs relating to them are included in section 06.

**Customer feasibility studies.** The company carries out paid feasibility studies for pharmaceutical customers. These use the company's established screening protocols, applied to the customer's substrate, and the results are reported to the customer. The studies are not R&D, because the protocols are established and the outcome is not a new advance in knowledge. Their revenue of £210,000 is disclosed in section 03. The staff time and consumables used on those studies have been excluded.

**GMP documentation.** The company prepares documentation for the manufacture of a future catalyst under good manufacturing practice. This work records and standardises procedures that have already been established, and it is not directed at resolving a technical uncertainty. Its costs have been excluded.

**Stock for feasibility jobs.** Enzymes, resins and other consumables drawn from the purchase ledger for customer feasibility studies have been excluded from the consumables claimed. The stock excluded on this basis amounts to £23,600.

**Other excluded costs.** In total the company has excluded £61,000 of costs that were recorded against the development project, the feasibility codes and the administration codes, but which relate to the activities described above. The exclusion is applied at source, so that the claimed figures in section 06 reflect only the R&D costs.

These boundaries have been applied consistently in the timesheets, the purchase ledger and the payroll apportionments described in section 06.
