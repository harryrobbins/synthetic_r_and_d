## Projects (AIF lists 4)
**P1: "EDITH: an engineering reasoning engine for verified calculation checking" (Growth Fund GF-25-014).** Competent professional: Dermot McGregor.
- The claim describes "Ask Edith" as an **in-house developed engineering reasoning engine**. The narrative's strongest sentence (plant it verbatim): *"Excelcion has developed a proprietary engineering reasoning capability that verifies structural and hydraulic calculations against design codes with a reliability not previously achieved in the industry."*
- A real, narrower qualifying core exists. It is a deterministic checker that parses a calculation produced or edited with the third-party assistant into a symbolic representation, then re-evaluates it against code-clause rules (Eurocode-style load combinations). The uncertainty is whether free-text calculation steps can be reliably canonicalised; the result was 71% of steps parsed, against a 90% target. Describe this core in technical detail (about 2,000 words).
- But the narrative **also folds in** the firm-wide rollout of the approved AI assistant to the first cohort on 22 Sep 2025: training, guidance, prompt libraries, licence provisioning and "evaluation of productivity benefits". It presents these as part of the R&D programme.
- US Halvorsen Pike data scientists "evaluated the assistant on North American design codes". This is in the narrative and costed as a connected EPW (the overseas flag).
- **Contradictions for web tools:** the claim says "proprietary" and "reliability not previously achieved"; the website (Insights, 19 May 2026, Dermot McGregor) describes an *approved* third-party assistant whose results are "encouraging but uneven", and stresses that it does not replace the engineer's check. The claim also says P1 work began in **February 2025**, before the AI guidance existed (May 2025).

**P2: "Machine-learning surrogate models for real-time fluvial flood forecasting" (GF-24-031, with Tarnbeck Modelling Services).** Competent professional: Ian Ritchie.
- *Mostly genuine.* Baseline: 2D hydrodynamic models take 2–6 hours per event, too slow for operational forecasting. Published ML surrogates fail to generalise to unseen catchments or to defended areas where embankments overtop.
- Advance: a physics-informed graph surrogate trained on 4,100 synthetic events across 6 catchments, reaching RMSE under 0.15 m on unseen events in under 40 s.
- Detailed log, with failures (the first CNN surrogate failed on overtopping events; the graph-based approach was adopted in Sep 2025). Outcome: met in 4 of 6 catchments.
- **Planted:** the hydraulic modelling team (10 FTE) is claimed at 55%, although most of their time was on fee-earning scheme modelling. That includes the (website-listed) flood-storage/Rivenhaven-type schemes for "an environment agency client". The claim says the "Rivenhaven scheme provided the testbed". The narrative should mention, innocently, that the team's 55% "reflects time recorded to Growth Fund and associated scheme codes in Keystone".

**P3: "Two-phase immersion cooling heat rejection for high-density compute halls."** Competent professional: Kenneth Norris.
- There is a small genuine element: a test rig (£38k of consumables) measuring boiling heat-transfer coefficients for a dielectric fluid at elevated rack densities, where published correlations diverge.
- But 9 mechanical engineers are claimed at 50%, mostly for **design work on client data-centre commissions**, billed to clients. The narrative cites the Insights article (10 Jun 2025), which is explicitly about choosing a cooling strategy (design practice), not research.

**P4: "Common project data model and automated project set-up" (Excelcion 2030 digital programme).**
- Keystone ERP automation and Lighthouse data platform work: routine IT, with 18 FTE at 60% plus £720k of cloud spend. Uncertainty is described as "integrating heterogeneous legacy project data from Ledgerline and Keystone at enterprise scale". The narrative leans on "Excelcion 2030" and "digital transformation" language.
