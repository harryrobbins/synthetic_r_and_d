## Projects (AIF lists 2)
**P1: Multipoint covalent immobilisation of an engineered ω-transaminase retaining >80% activity over 500 h in continuous flow (CVB-P1 "LONGFLOW").**
- Field: Biotechnology / biochemical engineering.
- Baseline: commercially available immobilised transaminases on epoxy-methacrylate resins lose 40–70% of activity within 100–150 h in packed-bed flow at 30–40 °C, through PLP cofactor leaching and subunit dissociation of the dimeric enzyme. Published approaches (glyoxyl-agarose multipoint attachment; co-immobilising PLP on polyethyleneimine coatings) improve stability but cut initial activity by 50–60% and are not scalable on rigid supports suitable for >5 bar. Name these approaches generically; give no real paper titles. Cite "published literature (refs 1–6 in Appendix B)" with fictional refs, e.g. "Hartigan & Moy, 2021, *J. Biocatal. Eng.*".
- Advance: a support chemistry plus enzyme surface-lysine engineering that keeps the dimer interface cross-linked, with PLP retained in situ. Target: >80% activity at 500 h, and initial activity ≥70% of free enzyme.
- Uncertainties: (1) whether the surface lysine mutations needed for oriented attachment would destabilise the dimer interface or the active-site geometry; (2) whether PLP could be retained without blocking the active site; (3) whether the pore architecture compatible with high-pressure flow would allow enough enzyme loading without diffusion limitation.
- Work log: build at least 18 dated entries across Apr 2024 – Mar 2025. Include **failures**: variant panel V1 (12 variants) gave 3 with acceptable stability but 2 aggregated during immobilisation; the PEI-PLP route was abandoned in Aug 2024 because of selectivity loss (ee fell from >99% to 94%); the switch to a hierarchical silica support was made in Oct 2024. By Mar 2025: 612 h at 83% retained activity, with initial activity at 74% of free enzyme. Partly resolved; a scale-up uncertainty remains open into FY2025-26.
- Boundary: starts 1 Apr 2024 (continuing from the FY23-24 feasibility work). The claim **explicitly excludes** the customer feasibility studies (paid work, routine screening using an established protocol) and the GMP documentation work.

**P2: Predicting immobilisation-induced activity loss from structure (CVB-P2 "PREDICTA").**
- Field: Computational biology.
- Baseline: structure-prediction and docking tools predict free-enzyme structure, but there is no validated method for predicting the activity retained after covalent multipoint attachment. Practitioners screen empirically.
- Advance: a surrogate model trained on the company's own 340-variant immobilisation dataset plus coarse-grained MD. Target: Spearman ρ > 0.6 on held-out variants.
- Uncertainties: whether a coarse-grained surface-accessibility signal carries enough information; whether the dataset size is sufficient.
- Outcome: honest partial success. ρ = 0.52 on holdout. The model is useful for pruning but did not meet the target. The project continues.
- Costs: GPU compute (£9,300) and the modelling licence belong to P2.
