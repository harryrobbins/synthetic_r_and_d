## Company
- Lantern Lane Joinery Ltd, company no. ZZ402291, UTR 9999991458. A bespoke kitchen and furniture maker with 4 people (2 directors, a joiner and an apprentice). Turnover £640k.
- Address: Unit 2, Lantern Lane Industrial Estate, Wexcombe, Dorsetshire ZZ9 4LL.
- Agent: **Quorlane R&D Partners Ltd** (the same as C04 and C05). It approached the company by cold call in May 2025 ("Did you know your business could be eligible for thousands in government tax credits?"). Reuse the boilerplate *verbatim*:
  > "Throughout the claim period the Company sought to achieve an appreciable improvement in the overall knowledge and capability in its field, resolving uncertainties that could not be readily deduced by a competent professional working in the field, in accordance with paragraphs 3, 6 and 13 of the DSIT Guidelines."

  AIF closing line: *"All activities were undertaken in a systematic, iterative and controlled manner, with outcomes recorded contemporaneously."*
  And this near-duplicate of the C05 sentence: "Prototypes were iteratively developed, tested against defined success criteria, and refined based on observed performance."
- Senior officer and competent professional: Craig Lantern, Director (City & Guilds Level 3 in Bench Joinery).
- **First-ever claim. No CNF was filed.** Section 02 should read: "Claim Notification: Not applicable. The Company has engaged with R&D tax relief for the first time this period and this claim is submitted together with the Company's CT600." This is wrong: a CNF *is* required for a first claim. The claim is therefore invalid. Do not comment on this.

## Projects (AIF lists 3, all non-qualifying)
1. **"Development of an innovative concealed-hinge bespoke larder system."** A larder unit using off-the-shelf hinges in a new layout.
2. **"Advanced CNC toolpath optimisation for hardwood components."** Learning to use a newly bought CNC router (the router itself, £38.5k, is claimed as "consumables").
3. **"Sustainable finishing techniques."** Switching to a commercially available water-based lacquer.

Uncertainties throughout are generic or business-related: "whether the design would meet customer expectations", "achieving a premium finish within budget", "minimising material waste". There are no baselines naming any existing product or method beyond "traditional joinery".

## Planted issues (flagrant)
- Director "salary" in the staff schedule includes **dividends** (Craig £48k, Michelle £30k). The cost section should say "Directors' remuneration (salary and dividends)" matter-of-factly.
- Michelle Lantern (company secretary, bookkeeping) is claimed at 80%.
- Timber for sold customer orders, van lease and fuel, and a CAD/CAM licence are all claimed.
- The claim value is high relative to the business (benefit about £34k on £640k turnover).
- The narrative is short and generic. It reads like a template with the nouns swapped ("joinery" substituted into sentences that also appear in C05). Keep the projects to about 900–1,200 words each.
- The evidence index lists "photographs of completed kitchens", "customer testimonials", "supplier invoices". Nothing is contemporaneous R&D documentation.
- Appendix: the declaration is signed by Craig Lantern. The agent's engagement letter excerpt shows a 30% fee and "no win, no fee". Include it, because it is a tell.
